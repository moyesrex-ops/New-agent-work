# CANDIDATE 1 — LEDGERWEDGE
### Compliant invoicing sold directly to the Nigerian micro-supplier

## SIM 1 — Problem frequency

**Trigger.** Emeka delivers ₦1.8m of railings to a bottler's facilities department and sends his usual photographed duplicate-book invoice to the procurement officer on WhatsApp.

**Frequency.** He invoices 8–20 times a month across all customers, but only 2–4 of those go to buyers in scope. So the *painful* event fires **2–4 times a month**, not 20. This matters: the pain is real but it is not daily, which caps habit formation.

**Next ten minutes after rejection.** He calls the procurement officer. He is told the invoice "must have the NRS number now." He does not know what that is. He asks his bank. He asks a friend with a bigger business. He types something into Google, or more likely he asks in a WhatsApp group. Elapsed time to a decision: `INFERRED` days, not minutes, because the rejection arrives by silence rather than by a bounce message — nobody tells him, the payment simply does not come.

**Cost of doing nothing.** ₦1.8m delayed indefinitely against a payroll of six. At the 28–32% informal borrowing rates documented for Lagos SMEs, bridging one month costs him roughly ₦45,000. Doing nothing for a quarter loses him the customer.

**Verdict:** severe when it fires, but low-frequency and *silently* triggered. Silent triggers are the enemy of self-serve acquisition. **7/10.**

## SIM 2 — Alternative autopsy

**Exact current stack:** duplicate invoice book (₦500), phone camera, WhatsApp, a bank app, and an "accountant" who is a cousin with a laptop who files something once a year.

**What he would move to, unprompted:** nothing. He would ask the *buyer* what to do.

**Real competitors, priced.** `OBSERVED` from vendor pages: FETCH at ₦9,999 Starter / ₦24,999 Growth / ₦59,999 Business per month; Moniepoint Moniebook at ₦6,000 Core / ₦8,500 Pro; Comply Invoice with a Play Store app and a stated "operational within 24 hours" onboarding; Duplo selling API-first to finance teams; Zoho Books with a Nigeria e-invoicing guide. Plus the **NRS self-service portal, free**, explicitly positioned for "small businesses, low invoice volume."

**Moment of maximum hatred.** Not the software — the *silence*. Three weeks after delivery, no money, no explanation, and a procurement officer who stops answering. That is the emotional peak, and it is 3 weeks *after* the moment a product could have helped.

**Switching cost.** Low in software terms, high in psychological terms: he must obtain and verify a TIN, understand that his invoice is now a government-cleared document, and trust an app with his customer list.

**Verdict:** the alternative is not another app, it is *free government software plus four funded competitors*. Competing on price against free, on convenience against Moniepoint's existing payment relationship, and on trust against nothing. **4/10.**

## SIM 3 — Discovery — ten paths, ranked

| # | Path | P(realistic) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer's procurement officer tells him what to get | High | **But the buyer names a vendor, and it will not be us** |
| 2 | Agent walks into his workshop | Medium-high | Costs ₦2,000–₦5,000 per activation |
| 3 | WhatsApp trade group | Medium | Real, unpurchasable, slow |
| 4 | Market association (MATAN, Alaba union) | Medium | Requires a partnership deal first |
| 5 | Another supplier who got paid | Medium | Best loop, but needs a base first |
| 6 | Bank or POS provider cross-sell | Low-medium | Moniepoint owns this and will use it against us |
| 7 | Google "NRS invoice for small business" | Low | Low search literacy in this segment |
| 8 | Play Store browse | Low | Over-credited channel |
| 9 | Accountant recommends | Low | Accountants recommend what they already use |
| 10 | Paid ads | — | **Penalised.** If this is the answer, the candidate is dead |

Path 1 is the highest-probability path and it *routes the user to a competitor named by the buyer*. That is a structural problem, not a marketing problem.

**Verdict: 5/10.**

## SIM 4 — First use, minute by minute

Assume he has the app.

- **0:00–0:20** Phone number, OTP. Already in his pocket: phone, SIM, bank app. Fine.
- **0:20–2:00** Business name, **TIN**. Here is the wall. If he does not know his TIN, the session ends. `INFERRED` a material share of this segment cannot produce a TIN from memory; TIN became mandatory for all businesses only in January 2026.
- **2:00–4:00** Customer details, including **the buyer's TIN**, which he almost certainly does not have and must request over WhatsApp. **Second wall, and it is external — it depends on someone else replying.**
- **4:00–6:00** Line items, HS codes, 7.5% VAT. HS code mapping is a genuine cognitive cliff for a fabricator.
- **6:00–?** Submit, wait for IRN.

**Trust obstacle:** "why is this app sending my sales to the government?" This is the single hardest objection in the Nigerian informal economy and it is entirely rational.

**Can a low-literacy user finish?** Only with help. **Not in one session, and not without the buyer's TIN.**

**Verdict: 4/10.** The first-run flow has an external dependency the user cannot resolve alone. That is close to disqualifying for a self-serve product.

## SIM 5 — Retention

Returns on day 2 only if he invoices again. Day 7: maybe. Day 30: only if the buyer relationship persists. There is money in motion, which is the strongest possible loop — but it fires 2–4 times a month and each firing is initiated by an external event.

The dangerous scenario: he uses it three times, wins the argument with the bottler, and then the bottler moves to net-90 anyway. The app stops being associated with getting paid and starts being associated with tax. Then he churns.

**Verdict: 5/10.** There is a loop, but it is thin and it competes with an incumbent that also holds his payments.

## SIM 6 — Willingness to pay — the Kippa test

Three hypotheses, then I attack them.

**H1: ₦3,000/month subscription.** *Attack:* Kippa had thousands of merchants on a genuinely loved bookkeeping product and could not convert them. The post-mortem line is not ambiguous: *"Useful doesn't always mean payable… anything that doesn't directly affect their cash flow quickly becomes 'I'll manage without it.'"* Compliance does affect cash flow — but only 2–4 times a month, and only for the subset with big customers. Against a free NRS portal. **Fails for most of the segment.**

**H2: ₦300 per validated invoice.** *Attack:* better aligned — he pays only when money is at stake. But at 3 in-scope invoices a month that is ₦900/month ARPU, against an agent activation cost of ₦2,000–₦5,000 and ongoing support in a segment that calls rather than reads. **Payback measured in years. Unit economics fail.**

**H3: The buyer pays on his behalf.** *Attack:* this is not C1. This is C2. **The strongest pricing hypothesis for C1 is that C1 should not exist.**

**Verdict: 3/10.** This is the decisive simulation for this candidate.

## SIM 7 — Liquidity / cold start

Single-player, so no marketplace cold start. Good. But no network effect either — supplier #500 makes supplier #501 no easier to acquire. Linear CAC forever.

**Verdict: 5/10.**

## SIM 8 — Trust and safety, assuming scammers arrive day 3

Realistic attacks: fake TIN entry to generate documents that look official; invoice-cloning fraud where a scammer issues invoices impersonating a real supplier to a real buyer; a "compliance agent" charging ₦20,000 to "register you," which is a scam that already exists around every Nigerian government process; harvesting the app's customer lists for competitor poaching.

Mitigations are real (TIN validation against the platform, device binding, rate limits) but the *impersonation* risk is nasty: because the product's output is designed to look official, forging it becomes valuable.

**Verdict: 5/10.**

## SIM 9 — Unit and growth math

All figures are **assumption ranges**, anchored where stated.

- Beachhead: Nigerian businesses with turnover ₦20m–₦1bn that supply at least one in-scope buyer. Anchor: 40m+ MSMEs total `REPORTED`; formal, TIN-holding, B2B-supplying firms are a small fraction. Estimate **150,000–400,000** `INFERRED`, wide.
- 24-month realistic penetration: 0.5–2% → **750–8,000 businesses**.
- Funnel: aware 100% → tried 12–20% → completed first invoice 35–50% (SIM 4's TIN walls) → active at day 30 20–30% → paid 15–25%.
- ARPU ₦900–₦3,000/month. Contribution margin: agent activation ₦2,000–₦5,000, support ~₦400/user/month, infra ~₦150.
- At ₦1,500 ARPU and ₦550 variable cost, contribution ≈ ₦950/month against ₦3,500 acquisition → **~3.7 month payback, but only on users who actually convert to paid**, which is 15–25% of triers. Blended CAC per *paying* user is ₦14,000–₦23,000 → **payback 15–24 months** in a market with 30%+ inflation.

**Verdict: 4/10.** Not obviously bankrupt, not remotely exciting, and inflation eats it.

## SIM 10 — Red team

**The attack:** "Users will not come. The enforcement date for this user is 2028. The ones who feel pain today feel it as *silence from a customer*, not as a search query. When they do act, they ask the buyer, and the buyer names its own vendor. You are selling ₦3,000/month software to a segment that a well-funded company already proved will not pay for software, against four competitors and a free government portal, with an agent channel whose cost exceeds a year of revenue. And Moniepoint can bundle this into Moniebook next quarter and give it away, because they make their money on payments."

**My answer:** the only counter is that none of the competitors are genuinely usable by a low-literacy user on a bad network, and that agent distribution plus market associations is a channel nobody else is working. That is a real edge. It is not enough to beat "the buyer names the vendor."

**The attack wins.**

# CANDIDATE 2 — TAILGATE
### Supplier-tail onboarding sold to the Nigerian large buyer

## SIM 1 — Problem frequency

**Two clocks, and they are very different.**

*Buyer's clock.* Ify's pain fires **monthly at VAT filing** and **continuously at invoice receipt**. Every inbound invoice without an IRN is a decision: reject it and delay a supplier who will show up at reception, or accept it and strand the VAT. Her AP team touches this **dozens of times a week**. And it spikes hard at month-end close and at audit.

*Supplier's clock.* Same 2–4 times a month as C1 — but now *invited*, not self-discovered.

**Trigger, precisely dated.** The NRS five-step compliance framework's step 5 is *"Ensure that all inbound B2B e-invoices received from vendors contain a valid Invoice Reference Number (IRN)"* `REPORTED` (Adeola Oyinlade & Co; Mondaq; and independently by a Nigerian tax practitioner on LinkedIn, 20 July 2026, describing it as "not a drill"). Large taxpayers had until 31 July 2026. **That date is five weeks in the past.**

**Next ten minutes after Ify's Financial Controller asks "what's our exposure?".** She opens the vendor master, filters for suppliers with no IRN activity, and counts. `INFERRED`: she will find hundreds and she will not have a plan.

**Cost of doing nothing.** The BusinessDay worked example: ₦52m of stranded VAT on ₦400m of VAT-inclusive purchases. Even discounting for the disputed statutory position, the *matching-stage* failure is agreed by both sides of that argument.

**Verdict: 9/10.** High-frequency, dated, monetised, and it lands on a named person with a boss.

## SIM 2 — Alternative autopsy

**Exact current stack:** a vendor master in SAP/Oracle/Sage/Dynamics; a spreadsheet of TINs with ~60% coverage `INFERRED`; circular letters to vendors; WhatsApp; an SI/APP contract that integrates *the buyer's own ERP* and stops at the boundary.

**The critical gap, stated in the source material.** The accredited SI/APP layer solves *"integrating the business's internal systems, such as Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems"* `OBSERVED` (NITDA guideline, via practitioner summary). Nothing in the SI's defined duty covers *your counterparty's* ability to issue. The 61 accredited providers are, by their own definitions, integrators of the buyer's stack.

**Real complaints, buyer side.** The advisory checklists tell Ify to *"Cleanse Customer & Vendor Data: Audit Tax Identification Numbers (TINs/Tax IDs) across supplier and client databases to prevent transmission rejection"* and *"Review Procurement Policies: Mandate that vendors supply valid IRNs on all commercial documentation"* `REPORTED`. Both are instructions to do enormous manual work with no tool named.

**Moment of maximum hatred.** The month-end reconciliation where the VAT return will not tie because 180 vendors sent unstamped PDFs, and the Financial Controller wants a number by Friday.

**Switching cost.** High once embedded in AP — which is the point.

**Verdict: 8/10.** The alternative is a spreadsheet and a circular. That is the best possible answer to this question.

## SIM 3 — Discovery — ten paths, ranked

| # | Path | P | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Big-4 / mid-tier tax advisory referral | High | These firms are publishing the checklists and have no product to sell |
| 2 | An accredited APP/SI reselling it to its own buyer clients | High | **Channel, not just competitor** — they have the buyer, we have the tail |
| 3 | LinkedIn among Nigerian tax managers | Medium-high | Observably an active, anxious professional community |
| 4 | NRS/CITN/ICAN stakeholder events | Medium-high | Mandated stakeholder engagement phases are literally scheduled |
| 5 | Direct outbound to named large taxpayers | Medium | ~200–400 targets. A list, not a market — that is a feature |
| 6 | Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN) | Medium | Agbara/Ogun corridor concentration |
| 7 | A supplier who used it tells their *other* buyer | Medium | **The compounding loop** |
| 8 | Bank transaction-banking teams | Low-medium | They own corporate relationships |
| 9 | Search | Low | Enterprise, not search-led |
| 10 | Paid ads | — | Not applicable. **No penalty incurred.** |

Paths 1, 2 and 5 are all workable with zero ad spend and a phone. Path 7 is the compounding asset.

**Verdict: 8/10.**

## SIM 4 — First use, minute by minute — both users

**Ify, first session (target: proof in one sitting).**
- **0:00–1:00** Signs in with work email. Uploads her vendor master CSV — the artefact she already has open.
- **1:00–2:00** The system matches vendors against TIN/CAC data and returns a single number: *"412 of your 612 vendors have never transmitted a compliant invoice. Estimated input VAT at risk this quarter: ₦38.4m."*
- **2:00–3:00** She selects 50 vendors and clicks *Invite*. WhatsApp and SMS go out in the buyer's name.
- **The signature moment:** she screenshots the exposure number and sends it to her Financial Controller. **That screenshot is the product's growth engine and it happens in session one.**

**Emeka, first session (target: under 2 minutes, one thumb).**
- **0:00–0:15** Taps a WhatsApp link from a customer he cannot annoy. Opens a web page — no install.
- **0:15–0:45** Phone number, OTP. **His business name, TIN and the buyer's TIN are already pre-filled from the buyer's vendor master.** The C1 killer wall is gone, because the buyer supplied both sides of the data.
- **0:45–1:30** Confirms details, enters amount and description. HS code is pre-defaulted from his vendor category, editable.
- **1:30–2:00** Submits. Gets a stamped, QR-bearing proof card. Shares it back to the customer on WhatsApp.

**Trust obstacle:** "why is this app sending my sales to the government?" — but now the answer is *"because your customer asked you to, and this is how you get paid,"* which is an answer he already accepts. The buyer's authority is doing work no marketing can buy.

**Verdict: 9/10.** The buyer's data removes the exact two walls that broke C1.

## SIM 5 — Retention

*Buyer:* returns every single month at VAT filing. Returns weekly for exception handling. The vendor master lives here. **This is the strongest retention in the Five** — it is embedded in a recurring statutory process with a penalty attached.

*Supplier:* returns whenever that buyer needs an invoice, then — critically — starts using it for *other* customers because he now has a working tool and a TIN on file. Cross-buyer usage is the leading indicator to watch.

**Verdict: 9/10.**

## SIM 6 — Willingness to pay

Who pays: the buyer. When: at contract, annually or monthly. Against what budget: the tax/compliance line that already funds Big-4 advisory retainers and SI/APP integration projects.

**H1: ₦2,000 per active supplier per month, ₦150,000/month floor.** A buyer with 400 tail vendors, 250 active → ₦500,000/month ≈ ₦6m/year. Against ₦38m of quarterly exposure. *Attack:* procurement will grind the per-supplier price. *Answer:* volume tiers, and the floor protects the small deals.

**H2: Flat ₦1.5m–₦4m/year per buyer entity.** Easier to sign, easier to budget, no per-unit haggling. *Attack:* leaves money on the table with a 2,000-vendor conglomerate. *Answer:* band by vendor count at renewal.

**H3: Free to the buyer; charge the supplier later once they use it for other customers.** *Attack:* this is C1 again, and C1 failed SIM 6. *Answer: rejected.* Do not reintroduce the loser's business model into the winner.

**The honest attack on all three:** the buyer's cheapest alternative is not a competitor, it is **terminating small vendors** and consolidating spend onto compliant larger ones. That is genuinely cheaper for the buyer and genuinely catastrophic for the supplier. It is also the strongest ethical argument *for* building this, and it goes into the ethics section rather than being spun as a moat.

**Verdict: 8/10.**

## SIM 7 — Liquidity / cold start

Not a marketplace. It is **single-player for the buyer from day one**: one buyer with 200 tail vendors is a complete, valuable deployment with no other buyer on the platform. That is the ideal cold-start shape.

The network effect is a bonus that accrues without being depended on: a supplier onboarded by Buyer A is already verified when Buyer B arrives, so buyer #10's tail is partly pre-populated. Concierge fallback for the first two deployments: the founding team manually onboards the first 100 suppliers per buyer, by phone, in Yoruba/Igbo/Pidgin as needed. That is affordable and it is how you learn.

**Verdict: 9/10.**

## SIM 8 — Trust and safety, assuming scammers arrive day 3

This is a money-and-identity product and the threat model is serious.

| Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fake supplier impersonates a real vendor to divert payment | Bank details are **read-only from the buyer's vendor master** and never editable in the supplier app. This is the single most important safety decision in the product. |
| Phishing the buyer-branded invitation | Invitations carry a buyer-specific code; the landing page shows the buyer's registered name and the last 4 of the vendor code; we never ask for a bank detail or a password |
| Supplier inflates an invoice | Buyer approves before transmission on high-value tiers; every action audit-logged |
| Replayed OTP / SIM swap | Device binding, OTP rate limits, re-verification on new device before any invoice submission |
| Insider at the buyer creates a fake vendor | Immutable audit log on vendor creation, exportable; segregation of the invite role from the vendor-create role |
| Forged proof card | Every card carries the NRS QR; verification is against the NRS, not against us. **We are not the source of truth and we should never pretend to be** |

The one that keeps me up: a "compliance agent" charging suppliers ₦20,000 to "register them" on a product that is free to them. Mitigation is loud, repeated, in-product copy — *this is free, nobody should ever charge you* — in the languages the fraud is committed in.

**Verdict: 7/10.** Hard, but the attack surface is bounded because we never move money and never own the truth.

## SIM 9 — Unit and growth math

Assumption ranges, anchored.

- **Buyer TAM:** large taxpayers (≥₦5bn) plus medium (₦1–5bn). Anchor: NRS bands. Estimate **1,500–4,000 entities** `INFERRED`. Serviceable Lagos/Ogun manufacturing + FMCG + construction beachhead: **300–600**.
- **24-month realistic penetration:** 3–8% of the beachhead → **10–48 buyers**.
- **Buyer funnel:** contacted 100% → meeting 25–40% (a named, dated, penalised obligation opens doors) → pilot 30–50% of meetings → paid conversion 40–60% of pilots.
- **Supplier funnel per buyer:** invited 100% → opened 45–65% (buyer's name on the message) → completed first invoice 55–75% (pre-filled data) → active at 60 days 40–55%.
- **Revenue:** 15 buyers × 250 active suppliers × ₦2,000 = **₦7.5m/month ≈ ₦90m/year (~$58k)** at month 24. Modest in dollars, healthy for a 4–8 person Lagos team.
- **Contribution:** infra + SMS/WhatsApp ≈ ₦180/supplier/month; support ≈ ₦250; gross margin ≈ **78%**.
- **CAC:** founder-led sales, 2 sellers, ~₦1.2m fully-loaded cost per closed buyer `INFERRED` → payback **~3–4 months** on a ₦400k/month account.

**Verdict: 8/10.** Not a rocket. A real, margin-positive business with a defensible position, which is what the brief asked for.

## SIM 10 — Red team

**The attack, and I will make it as strong as I can:**
1. *"This is enterprise sales on a $20,000 budget. You will spend nine months getting a first logo and run out of money."*
2. *"One of the 61 accredited providers ships this next quarter. They already have the buyer relationship."*
3. *"The buyer will just absorb the VAT or drop the small vendors. Both are cheaper than a project."*
4. *"You need HSM signing, Nigerian data residency and an SI accreditation. That is not a $20,000 app."*
5. *"The NRS could build supplier onboarding itself and give it away, like Ghana's GRA did."*

**My answers, honestly:**
1. Partly conceded. Mitigation: the first two buyers must be concierge pilots landed through advisory referral, and the $20,000 buys the *product*, not the sales cycle. The 90-day plan is built around this and it is the top risk in the register.
2. The real risk. But their product shape is ERP integration and their revenue model is per-buyer integration fees; a free supplier app is a cost centre to them. **More importantly, they are a distribution channel** — white-labelling to an APP is a legitimate route and it is in the plan.
3. Conceded that it happens sometimes. But absorbing ₦38m/quarter is a P&L conversation with a CFO, and dropping vendors breaks operations in ways procurement hates. This is the assumption I am least sure of, and it is A2 in the log.
4. Real. **Answer: v1 does not hold the licence.** Route through an already-accredited APP/SI as a white-label or partner, which is the ₦0 path, and apply for SI accreditation (₦1m fee + ₦10m paid-up capital ≈ $7k) once there is revenue. Data residency is solved by Lagos-hosted managed Postgres at ₦15,000–₦35,000/month `OBSERVED` (Vorynza pricing), or Rack Centre CloudOnGround, or Galaxy Backbone's naira-billed sovereign cloud.
5. Possible. Ghana's GRA did exactly this — and shipped software requiring *"a desktop or laptop, internet access, and a web browser."* Governments ship the portal; they do not ship the phone-first, offline-tolerant, buyer-integrated workflow. That is thin comfort but it is consistently true across every market in the scan.

**Does the attack win?** No — but attacks 1 and 2 draw blood and permanently shape the plan.

# CANDIDATE 3 — CLEARPASSAGE
### Recruitment-cost and contract proof for Nepali workers going to the Gulf

## SIM 1 — Problem frequency

Migration is a **once-every-two-to-four-years** event. The recruitment-fee decision happens **once**, over 4–12 weeks, and is irreversible. Trigger: a broker names a price. Next ten minutes: Bishnu calls a relative who went last year. Cost of doing nothing: NPR 200,000–500,000 and up to 4.5 months of foreign earnings, financed at ~27%.

Catastrophic severity, near-zero frequency. **4/10** — and frequency is not a detail, it is the whole retention problem.

## SIM 2 — Alternative autopsy

Stack: the broker (often a relative or village contact), the manpower agency, mandatory pre-departure orientation, Facebook returnee groups, eMigrate/FWCMS on the state side.

Maximum hatred: on arrival in Riyadh, discovering the contract is different and the wage is 40% lower. **That is after every possible intervention point.**

The autopsy finding that decides this candidate: pre-departure orientation centres *"have done little to curb recruitment fees, because these are paid much earlier in the recruitment process"* `REPORTED` (Guardian). And on the digital incumbents: they *"often function as state-sanctioned monopolies that digitize existing exploitative structures rather than providing transparent, direct access for workers."*

Switching cost: you cannot switch away from the person who has your passport and your deposit.

**Verdict: 3/10.**

## SIM 3 — Discovery

Pre-departure orientation is a genuine, mandatory congregation point — the one real strength here. But it occurs **after** the fee is paid. Returnee word of mouth is strong and free. Cooperative lenders are a plausible upstream channel and the only one that reaches the worker *before* the money moves. Agencies control the introduction and are structurally hostile.

Best realistic path: cooperative lenders + returnee networks. **5/10.**

## SIM 4 — First use

Language: Nepali, plus Maithili/Bhojpuri in the Terai corridors. Device: shared or basic Android. Literacy: variable.

Session: search employer/agency name → see reported fees paid by previous workers for the same destination and role → capture a photo of the offer letter and the promised terms → get a timestamped record.

It is buildable and it is genuinely useful *if* he opens it before paying. **6/10 on usability, and the whole candidate hinges on a timing problem the product cannot fix.**

## SIM 5 — Retention

Day 2: no reason. Day 7: no reason. Day 30: he is in Riyadh with a different SIM. Day 365: possibly, to file a grievance he has no power to win.

The only retention story is a remittance product bolted on — at which point this is a remittance startup competing with Remitly, and the ethics wrapper is decoration.

**Verdict: 2/10.**

## SIM 6 — Willingness to pay

Attacked in the dossier and it does not survive. (a) Charging a debt-bonded worker is indefensible. (b) Agency-paid trust badges hand the product to the abuser. (c) Gulf-employer ESG budgets exist post-2022 but that is an enterprise sale in Riyadh run from Kathmandu with a $20,000 budget. (d) Cooperative-lender underwriting is honest and tiny.

**Verdict: 2/10.**

## SIM 7 — Liquidity

Two-sided and both sides are cold: the data is only useful with thousands of returnee reports, and returnees have no incentive to file them. Fence: seed from published ILO/NSO survey microdata. Weak. **3/10.**

## SIM 8 — Trust and safety

The worst risk profile in the Five. A wrong "verified" badge sends someone into bonded labour. Agencies will astroturf reviews. Naming agencies invites defamation suits in a jurisdiction where 771 of 1,200 agencies have already faced action, meaning the sector is both litigious and connected. Worker data in the wrong hands is a retaliation risk.

**Verdict: 3/10** — and this is a moral load, not just a product one.

## SIM 9 — Unit and growth math

~700k–800k Nepali labour permits annually `INFERRED` from DoFE reporting. Even 5% reach = 35,000 users/year with **no payer** and **no repeat**. Any revenue model yields a rounding error, and the ESG-enterprise path has a 12–18 month sales cycle.

**Verdict: 2/10.**

## SIM 10 — Red team

**The attack:** "The fee is paid before your app exists in his life. Your channel is the abuser or a state monopoly. Your user never returns. Your payer is a Gulf employer you cannot reach. And if you get one badge wrong, a man ends up in a labour camp. This is an NGO programme, and a good one, but it is not a company — and dressing it as a startup will get it underfunded and then abandoned, which is worse than not starting."

**My answer:** I do not have one that survives. The honest response is that this problem needs enforcement and bilateral labour agreements, not an app, and that the useful private-sector contribution is probably fair-recruitment auditing sold to Gulf employers — a consultancy, not a $20,000 product.

**The attack wins decisively.**

| P(10,000 active in 18 months) | 25–35% |
| P(paid usage) | **5–10%** |
| Revised rubric (of 150) | **51** |
| **Decision** | **KILL.** Stated plainly: this is the most important problem in the Five and the one I am least able to solve with software and $20,000. Killing it for the right reason is better than shipping a gesture. |

# CANDIDATE 4 — OWNPIPE
### Demand independence for US home-services contractors

## SIM 1 — Problem frequency

Marcus feels it **weekly** when the Angi invoice posts, and **daily** when a lead ghosts. High frequency, moderate-to-severe cost: at $14,000/year against eleven closed jobs, his effective cost per booked job is $250–$500+ `REPORTED`.

Trigger: a $75 charge for a lead with a disconnected number. Next ten minutes: he disputes it, the dispute is denied or slow-credited, he posts in r/HVAC.

**Verdict: 7/10.** Frequent and financially quantified — genuinely good.

## SIM 2 — Alternative autopsy

Stack: Angi/HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, sometimes Google LSA, a Facebook page, a nephew's website, Jobber or Housecall Pro for scheduling.

Maximum hatred is well documented and quotable: *"2/3 were fraudulent. Bogus names, dead numbers. ROI = $0."* And the lock-in: *"To cancel they demanded 35% of remainder of year."*

**But the autopsy also reveals the problem with the candidate.** The market has already found the answer and it is not a startup: r/HVAC's own advice is *"use Angi for immediate volume while building your Google LSA and Google Business Profile presence, then shift"* `REPORTED`. Google LSA delivers **exclusive** leads at $39–$59 in home services — cheaper and better than Angi, from a company Marcus already trusts.

**Verdict: 5/10.** The hatred is 9/10; the whitespace is 3/10, because Google already ate it.

## SIM 3 — Discovery

Genuinely good: r/HVAC, r/sweatystartup, ContractorTalk, named Facebook groups, supply-house counters, and high-intent search on "Angi alternative." Word of mouth in trades is strong.

But conversion from those channels needs content and SEO investment, and the eventual scaled channel is paid search — which the brief penalises, correctly.

**Verdict: 7/10.**

## SIM 4 — First use

English, iPhone or modern Android, good network, business email. No accessibility or literacy constraints. Easy to build a lovely first session: connect Google Business Profile, see a scorecard, get three fixes.

**Verdict: 8/10** — the easiest first-use of the Five, and that ease is exactly why it is competitive.

## SIM 5 — Retention

Returns weekly if leads arrive. The killer: **attribution**. If Marcus cannot see that the booked job came from us rather than from a neighbour's referral, he churns at the first slow month. And home services is seasonal.

**Verdict: 6/10.**

## SIM 6 — Willingness to pay

Excellent and proven — he already pays $14,000/year. $99–$249/month is easy to justify **if it works**.

Three hypotheses: flat SaaS ($149/mo) — attacked by "did it work this month?"; performance ($50 per booked job) — attacked by attribution disputes, which are exactly why Angi is hated; hybrid ($99 + $25/booking) — best, still attribution-dependent.

**Verdict: 8/10** on willingness, **5/10** on the durability of the value claim.

## SIM 7 — Liquidity

If it is a marketplace, homeowner supply is the cold start and that is a nightmare against Angi's spend. If it is a tool (GBP optimisation, review generation, missed-call text-back, LSA management) there is no cold start — but then it is software plus services, i.e. an agency.

**Verdict: 5/10.**

## SIM 8 — Trust and safety

Comparatively benign: review-gating is an FTC issue and must be avoided; fake reviews violate Google policy and would be fatal; homeowner PII needs care. No money movement, no identity documents.

**Verdict: 8/10.**

## SIM 9 — Unit and growth math

~700k+ US home-services firms `INFERRED`. Beachhead Phoenix + Dallas HVAC/plumbing/electrical 1–3 trucks: **8,000–15,000** firms. 24-month penetration 1–3% → **80–450 customers**. At $149/mo → **$14k–$67k MRR**. CAC via content/community $300–$800; via paid search $1,200–$2,500. LTV at 14-month average life ≈ $2,000. Workable on organic CAC; marginal on paid.

**Verdict: 6/10.**

## SIM 10 — Red team

**The attack:** "Google Local Services Ads already solved this: exclusive leads, cheaper, from a brand he trusts, with the Google Guarantee badge. Your product is Google Business Profile management with a dashboard, which is an agency. Jobber and Housecall Pro can add it as a feature. There is no mandate, no deadline, no counterparty withholding money — Marcus can keep complaining about Angi for another decade, and he will. You are selling a vitamin to a man who is loudly describing his headache but has already been handed aspirin."

**My answer:** the anger is real, the budget is real, and the execution gap between "Google LSA exists" and "Marcus has it configured well" is where the business lives. That is a genuine services business. It is not structural pull.

**The attack substantially wins.**

| P(10,000 active in 18 months) | **10–15%** — beachhead too small, penetration too slow |

# CANDIDATE 5 — HOLDFAST
### Neutral custody and proof for Lagos rent deposits and tenancy

## SIM 1 — Problem frequency

**Once a year.** Sometimes once every two years. Severity is very high — ₦400,000+ at risk, roughly 37.5% agency plus 37.5% legal plus a ₦100,000 "refundable" deposit plus ₦15,000 viewing fees, against a ₦400,000 rent `REPORTED`.

Trigger: an agent says "pay today or lose it." Next ten minutes: **he pays**, because Lagos housing supply is scarce and hesitation loses the flat. That is the whole problem — the decision window is minutes and the fear of losing the property beats the fear of losing the money.

**Verdict: 4/10.** Maximum severity, minimum frequency, and a decision window too short for a considered product.

## SIM 2 — Alternative autopsy

Stack: cash or bank transfer, a Word tenancy agreement, an unregistered agent, and hope. LASRERA exists and is trying — registration compulsory since June 2025, complaint lines published, fee caps in law — and is comprehensively ignored: *"Lagos tenants pay double rent cost as agents defy 10% rule"* `REPORTED` (Nairametrics, Oct 2025).

Maximum hatred: eviction time, when the deposit is not returned.

**The structural problem:** the party who must accept custody (the landlord/agent) is the party who benefits from no custody. There is no version where the fraudster opts into escrow.

**Verdict: 4/10.**

## SIM 3 — Discovery

Employer HR relocation packs are a real, credible channel for the salaried Yaba/Lekki segment. LASRERA co-branding would be powerful if obtainable. WhatsApp house-hunting groups are where the market actually runs.

But every path reaches the *tenant*, and the tenant cannot unilaterally impose custody.

**Verdict: 5/10.**

## SIM 4 — First use

Tenant: smartphone, banked, literate — easy. Landlord/agent: often older, often cash-preferring, frequently unregistered, and being asked to accept delayed funds from a stranger's app. **The hard user is the one with all the power.**

**Verdict: 4/10.**

## SIM 5 — Retention

Annual. The only genuine returns are rent-renewal reminders and a dispute six to twenty-four months later. No habit.

**Verdict: 2/10.**

## SIM 6 — Willingness to pay

1–2% of deposit = ₦4,000–₦8,000 on a ₦400,000 deposit. Tenant would plausibly pay for peace of mind at the moment of maximum fear. Landlord would not pay a naira. Float income requires holding client money, which requires a CBN licence.

**Verdict: 4/10.**

## SIM 7 — Liquidity

Two-sided, and the required side is hostile. Fence options: partner with a small number of professional letting agencies who *want* legitimacy (real, but they are not where the fraud is); or ride an employer mandate. Both are slow.

**Verdict: 3/10.**

## SIM 8 — Trust and safety

Custodying other people's money in a high-fraud market: CBN licensing, segregated accounts, AML, and a dispute-adjudication function with legal capability. Fake landlords will try to receive custody releases. This is an operations and compliance business.

**Verdict: 3/10** for a $20,000 build. Not a criticism of the idea — a statement about the budget.

## SIM 9 — Unit and growth math

Lagos formal-ish annual tenancy transactions in the target segment: **200,000–500,000** `INFERRED`, wide and weakly grounded. 24-month penetration 0.3–1% → 600–5,000 transactions. At ₦6,000 → **₦3.6m–₦30m/year (~$2k–$19k)**. Before licensing costs. Float is the real revenue and float requires the licence.

**Verdict: 3/10.**

## SIM 10 — Red team

**The attack:** "The landlord will not accept escrow, and he does not have to, because ten people want the flat. Your user transacts once a year, so there is no habit and no compounding. You need a CBN licence to hold the money that is the entire point of the product. And LASRERA already exists, already has the legal power, and is already being ignored — if the *government* cannot make agents comply, a startup with $20,000 will not."

**My answer:** the only real one is a mandate — a large employer or a statutory scheme like the UK's, where deposit protection is compulsory and the schemes became infrastructure by law. That is a policy campaign with a product attached, and the sequencing is wrong for a first version.

**The attack wins.**

| P(paid usage) | 30–40% |
