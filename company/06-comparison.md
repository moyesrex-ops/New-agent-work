## 5.2 Head-to-head on user pull, not founder romance

The only question that matters: **why does a user show up this quarter without being advertised to?**

| Candidate | The single sentence that gets a user through the door | Is it structural? |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | "My customer stopped paying me and I don't know why." | Partly — but the trigger is *silence*, and silence does not route to a product |
| **C2** | "My biggest customer sent me a WhatsApp link and said use this or we can't pay you." **And:** "My Financial Controller asked what our stranded VAT exposure is and I have no answer." | **Yes. Two named, dated, monetised parties, both already in scope** |
| C3 | "A friend said check this before you pay the broker." | No — the money moves before the app enters the story |
| C4 | "I'm sick of Angi and I saw this on r/HVAC." | No — genuine anger, but he can suffer indefinitely, and Google already sells the fix |
| C5 | "I don't want to lose ₦400,000." | No — and the counterparty who must consent has every reason to refuse |

**C1 vs C2 — settling the strategic question.** Same mandate, same city, same underlying document. The difference is who is coerced *now*. The supplier's enforcement date is 2028; the buyer's was 31 July 2026. Selling to the party whose deadline has already passed beats selling to the party whose deadline is two years away — and it happens to fix the supplier's product experience for free, because the buyer supplies the two data fields (his TIN, the buyer's TIN) that broke C1's first-run flow. **C2 does not merely beat C1; C2 contains C1 and repairs it.**

**C2 vs C4 — the real contest.** C4 wins on willingness to pay and on ease of building. It loses on everything structural. Marcus has been angry at Angi for years and will be angry next year too; nothing forces his hand on a date. Ify has a deadline that passed five weeks ago and a Financial Controller asking for a number. That is the difference between a market and a mood.

## 5.3 Sensitivity — if one key assumption breaks, who still lives?

| Shock | C1 | **C2** | C3 | C4 | C5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nigeria defers the whole mandate by 2 years** (the Malaysia scenario) | Dies | **Wounded, survives.** Large-taxpayer obligation is already live and enforced; a deferral would protect *small suppliers* from the tax office, not buyers from their own filings. And the product pivots to Ghana or Kenya where enforcement is live | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| **Malaysia-style political retreat: threshold raised so small suppliers are formally exempt** | Dies outright | **Survives, arguably strengthens.** If suppliers are exempt, they will *never* self-serve, so the only way a buyer gets clean documents is if the buyer pays for it | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| An accredited APP ships supplier-tail onboarding | Dies | **Hurt badly.** Mitigation: white-label to them instead of competing. This is the top competitive risk | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Google launches a contractor CRM with LSA bundled | n/a | n/a | n/a | **Dies** | n/a |
| Regulator declines to endorse a deposit scheme | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | **Dies** |
| Gulf employers decline to fund ethical-recruitment verification | n/a | n/a | **Dies** | n/a | n/a |
| Naira devalues another 40% | Dies (₦3,000 ARPU evaporates) | **Survives** — buyer contracts can be repriced annually and the value is denominated in VAT saved, which inflates with prices | n/a | n/a | Hurt |
| Founding team cannot close an enterprise deal | Survives (self-serve) | **Dies.** This is C2's real single point of failure | n/a | Survives | Dies |

**Reading of the sensitivity table.** C2 survives every shock except one: *the team cannot sell to a corporate.* Every other candidate dies to at least one shock that is outside the team's control. C2's fatal risk is inside the team's control, which is the correct place for a fatal risk to be.

The Malaysia row is the most interesting result in this memo. The political retreat that kills C1 **strengthens** C2, because exemption removes the supplier's own reason to act while leaving the buyer's obligation untouched. A thesis that gets stronger under the most likely adverse political scenario is a thesis worth backing.

# Phase 6 — Forced Decision

## 6.1 The sentence

> **If I could only back one of these, I back TAILGATE — the supplier-tail compliance product sold to the Nigerian large buyer — because users are structurally pulled toward it by a legal obligation that took effect on 31 July 2026 requiring large taxpayers to receive only IRN-bearing invoices, by an input-VAT exposure that a named finance manager must explain to her Financial Controller every month, and by a supplier who arrives via a WhatsApp link from a customer he cannot afford to annoy rather than via an advertisement; and the simulations showed a 9/10 first-use because the buyer supplies the exact two data fields (the supplier's TIN and the buyer's TIN) that made Candidate 1's first session impossible to complete, 9/10 retention because the buyer returns every month at VAT filing, and survival under every sensitivity shock except the team's own inability to sell. The others lose on: Ledgerwedge on willingness to pay (3/10 — the Kippa trap, verbatim), Clearpassage on the fact that recruitment fees are paid before any app enters the worker's life, Ownpipe on the absence of any structural pull plus Google Local Services Ads having already sold the fix, and Holdfast on a once-a-year frequency and a counterparty who must consent to custody and never will.**

## 6.2 Winner package

### 1. Working title
**TAILGATE** (working only — naming is Phase 7).

### 2. The problem, in one paragraph a skeptical operator would accept

Nigeria operates a clearance-model e-invoicing system: an invoice becomes a legally valid tax invoice only when the Nigeria Revenue Service validates it and returns an Invoice Reference Number and cryptographic stamp. As of 31 July 2026 every large taxpayer (≥₦5bn turnover) must, per the NRS's own five-step compliance framework, *receive only invoices bearing a valid IRN from its suppliers*; medium taxpayers (₦1–5bn) went live 1 July 2026. Those companies can integrate their own ERP through one of 61 accredited providers. What none of them can do is integrate the long tail of small vendors — the caterer, the haulier, the printer, the fabricator — who have no software, no accountant, and no enforcement deadline of their own until 2028. Every invoice from that tail is now either input VAT the buyer cannot recover or a payment the buyer cannot make, and the current remedy is a circular letter and an AP clerk chasing vendors on WhatsApp. India ran this exact experiment three years earlier and the outcome was documented: *"the buyer's accounts payable team withholds payment or demands rectification, placing commercial pressure on the small supplier that the GST law, by itself, does not impose."*

### 3. Why this is crucial, not merely interesting

Because the alternative outcome is already visible and it is ugly. The buyer's cheapest response to a non-compliant vendor tail is to **stop buying from small vendors** and consolidate spend onto larger compliant suppliers. That is a rational procurement decision and it would quietly de-formalise thousands of Nigerian micro-businesses out of the corporate supply chain at exactly the moment the state is trying to formalise them. This product's social function is to make keeping the small vendor cheaper than dropping them. That is not a marketing line; it is the actual competitive alternative identified in SIM 6.

### 4. First user, first geography, first channel

- **First paying user:** the Tax Manager / Financial Controller at a ₦5bn–₦50bn manufacturer or FMCG business in the Lagos–Agbara–Ogun corridor with 300+ vendors and a physical, messy tail.
- **First free user:** that buyer's small vendors, arriving by WhatsApp invitation.
- **First channel:** mid-tier tax advisory firms (who are publishing compliance checklists with no product to sell), then the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria, then direct outbound to a named list of roughly 200–400 in-scope entities.

### 5. Why users come even if marketing is mediocre

The buyer comes because a deadline has passed and someone senior asked for a number. The supplier comes because their customer sent the link and their money depends on it. **Neither arrival path involves us being persuasive.** The only marketing skill required is finding the buyer; the supplier is delivered by the buyer's own authority.

### 6. Product wedge

One job: **turn a small vendor's invoice into an IRN-bearing compliant invoice in under two minutes on a cheap Android phone with bad network — using data the buyer already has.** Plus the buyer console that makes the tail visible and inviteable. Nothing else in v1.

### 7. Proof from analogs and complaints

- India: buyers built IRN validation into vendor portals; *"The fix is not a vendor problem. It is a portal design problem."*
- Kenya: eTIMS created a **buyer-initiated invoicing** provision for suppliers under KES 5m — legal recognition that the buyer should be able to originate on the small supplier's behalf. No consumer-grade product exists for it.
- Nigeria: the NRS five-step framework's step 5, and advisory guidance instructing buyers to *"Cleanse Customer & Vendor Data"* and *"Mandate that vendors supply valid IRNs"* with no tool named.
- Kippa's post-mortem establishing that the supplier will not pay, which is why the supplier is not the customer.

Weakest: wedge scale 7 (the buyer market is a list of hundreds, not thousands) and whitespace 7 (61 accredited providers, four SME invoicing apps, one of whom could pivot).

### 11. Kill criteria — explicit, dated, and I will honour them

Kill or hard-pivot if **any** of these is true:

1. **Month 4:** no buyer has signed a paid contract *and* fewer than two pilots are running.
2. **Month 6:** supplier completion rate for first invoice is below 35% despite pre-filled data — meaning the product does not work for the actual user.
3. **Month 6:** an accredited APP has launched equivalent supplier-tail onboarding *and* refused a white-label partnership.
4. **Month 9:** buyer gross churn above 20%, indicating the exposure fear was a one-off audit panic and not a recurring need.
5. **Any time:** NRS formally suspends the requirement for large taxpayers to receive IRN-bearing invoices (not a deferral for small taxpayers — that is survivable — but removal of the *buyer's* obligation).
6. **Any time:** we discover buyers are systematically dropping small vendors rather than onboarding them, at a rate that makes the product's premise false.

### 12. Second best, and why it lost

**Ownpipe (C4), 96/150.** Real anger, real budget, easy build, benign safety profile, and it loses on the only thing that cannot be manufactured: nothing forces the contractor's hand on a date, and Google Local Services Ads already sells the remedy with better economics and a brand he trusts. A business, not a structurally-pulled one.

### 13. Ethics constraints

This product sits between a powerful buyer and a weak supplier. That asymmetry has to be governed, not admired.

1. **The supplier never pays.** Not a launch promise, a permanent constraint. If the supplier is ever charged for the core compliance job, the product has become an extraction device against the least powerful party in the chain.
2. **The supplier owns their data and can leave with it.** Invoice history exports to PDF and CSV without asking permission. No hostage-taking.
3. **We never let the buyer edit the supplier's bank details, and we never let the supplier edit them either.** Bank data is read-only from the buyer's vendor master. This closes the single most dangerous fraud vector and it also prevents us becoming a payment-diversion tool.
4. **No lending to suppliers in v1, and no selling supplier data to lenders, ever.** The obvious year-two move — invoice factoring against IRN-validated invoices — is legitimate only if the supplier opts in explicitly and can decline without losing platform access. Nigerian digital-lending harassment is a documented harm and this platform will not feed it.
5. **We will not build supplier-scoring that buyers can use to deselect small vendors.** The product exists to keep small vendors in the chain. A "compliance risk score" would be a delisting weapon. Refused.
6. **Loud, repeated, multilingual anti-scam copy:** *this is free, nobody should ever charge you to register.* Because someone will try.
7. **We are never the source of truth.** Verification is against the NRS QR, not against us. If we disappear, every proof card a supplier holds still verifies.

### 15. Metrics dictionary — vanity versus truth

**North-star action:** *A supplier who was not previously compliant transmits an invoice that receives a valid IRN, to a buyer on the platform.* Event name: `supplier_invoice_irn_issued`.

Not "signups." Not "invoices created." The IRN is the moment value exists, because it is the moment the buyer can deduct and the supplier can be paid.

| Vanity | Truth | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Registered suppliers | **Suppliers with ≥1 IRN-issued invoice in the last 30 days** | Registration is the buyer's action, not the supplier's |
| Invoices created | **Invoices that received an IRN** | Created-but-failed is a product defect, not usage |
| Buyer logins | **Buyers whose monthly VAT filing referenced platform data** | The filing is the job |
| Total suppliers on platform | **Suppliers active with ≥2 distinct buyers** | The only real measure of the network effect |
| "Exposure identified" | **Exposure closed** — VAT recoverable this quarter that was not last quarter | The number the CFO cares about |
| App store rating | **Median seconds from invite-open to first IRN** | The product's actual promise |
| MRR | MRR **and** logo count **and** net revenue retention | A single ₦2m logo can hide five churns |

Supporting metrics: supplier first-invoice completion rate; median time-to-IRN; IRN failure rate by error code; supplier 30/60/90-day retention; buyer gross and net churn; suppliers per buyer; cross-buyer supplier rate.
