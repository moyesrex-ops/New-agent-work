# Phase 3 — The Five

Working titles only. Naming is Phase 7 and does not start until a winner exists.

## 3.1 The Five and how they differ

| | C1 — LEDGERWEDGE | C2 — TAILGATE | C3 — CLEARPASSAGE | C4 — OWNPIPE | C5 — HOLDFAST |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Beachhead geography** | Lagos, Nigeria | Lagos, Nigeria | Nepal → Saudi/UAE/Qatar | Phoenix / Dallas, USA | Lagos, Nigeria |
| **User type** | Micro-supplier owner | Corporate AP/tax manager | Departing migrant worker | 1–3 truck trade contractor | Urban renter + landlord |
| **Problem type** | Compliance | Counterparty risk + working capital | Debt bondage / exploitation | Extractive intermediary | Fraud / trust custody |
| **Business model** | Supplier subscription | Buyer pays per active supplier | Unclear — the core problem | Contractor subscription | Float + custody fee |
| **Distribution motion** | Agents + market associations | Enterprise land-and-expand, buyer invites supplier | Pre-departure orientation centres | Search + trade forums | Regulator + employer HR |

Differences: **all five** differ from each other on at least four of the five dimensions. C1 and C2 share only geography — they are the two opposite answers to the single strategic question this memo has to settle: *when a mandate creates pain on both sides of a transaction, which side do you sell to?* I refuse to answer that by assertion, so both are simulated in full.

**Stated limitation.** Nigeria is the beachhead for three of five. That is not a diversity failure I am hiding — it is where my primary research is deepest, and C5 shares nothing with C1/C2 except a city. But it does mean the memo is more exposed to a single-country political shock than I would like, and that exposure is priced in Phase 5 sensitivity.

## 3.2 Dossier — C1 "LEDGERWEDGE"
### Compliant invoicing sold directly to the Nigerian micro-supplier

**Problem narrative.** Nigeria's Merchant Buyer Solution runs a clearance model: an invoice is not legally a tax invoice until the NRS validates it and returns an Invoice Reference Number and a cryptographic stamp. Large taxpayers hit full compliance on 31 July 2026, medium taxpayers went live 1 July 2026, and both are required to receive only IRN-bearing invoices. A supplier below ₦1bn is not enforced until 2028 — but the supplier's *customer* is enforced now. The supplier discovers this when an invoice bounces.

**Specific user portrait.** Emeka, 41. Runs a six-person aluminium fabrication workshop off Oshodi–Apapa Expressway. Turnover roughly ₦60m. Supplies window frames and railings to two building contractors and, since last year, directly to a beverage bottler's facilities department. Android phone, 4GB RAM, running a two-year-old Android build. Data bundle, not unlimited. Writes invoices in a duplicate book and photographs them for WhatsApp. Has a TIN because his bank made him get one. Has never opened TaxProMax. Reads English fine; would not describe himself as a computer person. His wife does the arithmetic.

**Job to be done.** *"Give my customer a document their accounts department will accept, today, so they release my money."*

**Beachhead.** Lagos mainland light-manufacturing and trade suppliers with ₦20m–₦500m turnover who already sell to at least one ₦1bn+ buyer.

**Acquisition loop.** Agents recruited from the POS-agent pool work market clusters and industrial estates on commission per activated supplier. Market associations — MATAN (present in all 774 LGAs, claims 40m+ members, already partnered with FIRS on the VAT Direct Initiative), Alaba Amalgamated Traders Union, ASMATA — provide bulk introductions. Word of mouth after a supplier's invoice gets paid.

**Monetization.** ₦2,500–₦5,000/month, or ₦200–₦500 per validated invoice. Positioned against FETCH's ₦9,999 Starter and Moniebook's ₦6,000 Core.

**Alternatives today.** The NRS self-service portal (free, desktop-shaped, low-volume mode); Comply Invoice; FETCH; Duplo; Zoho Books; an accountant at ₦20k–₦50k/month; and doing nothing until the invoice bounces.

**Unfair-advantage hypothesis.** Being the only compliance tool a low-literacy user can complete in 90 seconds on a bad network, distributed by agents nobody else is paying.

**Fatal risks.** (1) **This is the Kippa trap wearing a compliance hat** — the same user, the same price point, the same "useful but not payable" dynamic. (2) At least four funded Nigerian competitors already sell exactly this to exactly this user. (3) Cost to serve a ₦3,000/month customer with agent-assisted onboarding is likely negative. (4) The supplier's enforcement date is 2028; only the *lucky* ones with big customers feel pain now, and they are a minority of the addressable base.

**Evidence that would raise confidence:** a measurable spike in supplier-side signups timed to buyer circulars. **Evidence that would kill it:** any competitor publishing sub-₦3,000 pricing with an agent channel.

## 3.3 Dossier — C2 "TAILGATE"
### Supplier-tail onboarding sold to the Nigerian large buyer

**Problem narrative.** Same mandate, opposite end. A Nigerian large taxpayer's fifth compliance obligation, in the NRS's own five-step framework, is: *"Ensure that all inbound B2B e-invoices received from vendors contain a valid Invoice Reference Number (IRN)."* Big companies can integrate their own ERP. What they cannot do is integrate four hundred small vendors — the caterer, the diesel supplier, the haulage owner-operator, the cleaning contractor, the printer, the borehole guy. Every one of those vendors' invoices is now either a stranded-VAT liability or an unpayable invoice, and the AP team is chasing them one by one over WhatsApp. India solved this by bolting IRN validation into enterprise vendor portals; the fix was described there as *"not a vendor problem. It is a portal design problem."*

**Specific user portrait.** Two users, and both matter.
*Buyer-side:* Ify, 34, Tax Manager at a ₦40bn-turnover FMCG manufacturer in Agbara. Reports to a Financial Controller who has asked her, in writing, how much input VAT is at risk. She has a spreadsheet of 612 vendors, has TIN data for maybe 60% of them, and has sent two circulars that mostly went unread. She is measured on the audit not blowing up.
*Supplier-side:* the same Emeka from C1 — but he arrives via a WhatsApp link from a customer he cannot afford to annoy, not via an agent, and he pays nothing.

**Job to be done.** Buyer: *"Make my vendor tail stop producing invoices I can't deduct, without me hiring three people."* Supplier: *"Do what my customer just asked me to do so I get paid."*

**Beachhead.** The ~200–400 Nigerian entities in the NRS large-taxpayer band plus the medium band that went live 1 July 2026, starting with manufacturers and FMCG in Agbara, Ikeja and Ogun corridor — long, messy, physical vendor tails.

**Acquisition loop.** Sell to the buyer. The buyer invites the supplier. **CAC on the supplier side is structurally zero and the invitation carries the buyer's authority, which is the only authority that works on a Nigerian micro-supplier.** Each onboarded supplier is then discoverable by the next buyer, so buyer #2's tail is partly pre-populated. That is a real, compounding liquidity asset and it is the only genuine network effect in the Five.

**Monetization.** Buyer pays per active supplier per month (hypothesis: ₦1,500–₦3,000, floor of ₦150k/month) — priced against ₦52m of stranded VAT on ₦400m of purchases, which is not a close call. Supplier free forever on the core job.

**Alternatives today.** Circulars and WhatsApp chasing; the buyer absorbing the VAT; the buyer terminating small vendors and consolidating onto compliant larger ones (**the ugliest and most likely alternative — and it is also an argument that this product is ethically necessary**); enterprise SI/APP integration projects that address the buyer's own ERP but not the tail.

**Unfair-advantage hypothesis.** Three layers. (1) Workflow lock-in inside the buyer's AP process. (2) An accumulating verified-supplier graph — TIN, bank, contact, compliance status — that gets more valuable with each buyer. (3) A NITDA System Integrator accreditation, which costs ₦1m application plus ₦10m paid-up share capital — roughly $7,000 all-in at current rates, *inside* the $20,000 budget — and which no unaccredited competitor may legally operate without.

**Fatal risks.** (1) Enterprise sales cycles versus a $20,000 budget and a 6–10 week build. (2) One of the 61 accredited providers ships supplier-tail onboarding first. (3) The buyer decides absorbing the VAT is cheaper than a project. (4) HSM-backed signing and Nigerian data residency are genuine cost floors, not hand-waves. (5) You must be good at two very different products at once: an enterprise console and a low-literacy phone app.

**Evidence that would raise confidence:** named large Nigerian corporates issuing supplier IRN circulars; any of them building a vendor portal for it. **Evidence that would kill it:** an accredited APP already selling per-supplier onboarding to buyers.

## 3.4 Dossier — C3 "CLEARPASSAGE"
### Recruitment-cost and contract proof for Nepali workers going to the Gulf

**Problem narrative.** A Nepali worker is legally liable for an NPR 10,000 service fee and actually pays NPR 200,000–500,000, financed at around 27% annual interest, sometimes against land. It takes an average 3.3 months of earnings to recover recruitment costs, 4.5 months for women. Recruitment fees account for roughly 15% ($5.6bn) of the illegal profits from international migrant labour, and about 20% of all forced-labour cases arise from debt bondage. Nepal's Department of Foreign Employment has acted against 771 of roughly 1,200 active manpower agencies.

**Specific user portrait.** Bishnu, 26, from Nawalparasi. Sold buffalo and borrowed from a cooperative at 24% to pay NPR 320,000 to a broker who is a cousin's friend, for a construction job in Riyadh at a promised SAR 1,600/month. He will sign a contract in Kathmandu, and a different one in Riyadh.

**Job to be done.** *"Let me find out before I borrow whether this offer and this agency are real, and hold proof of what I was promised."*

**Beachhead.** Pre-departure orientation centres in Kathmandu, plus the Nawalparasi–Rupandehi–Jhapa recruitment corridors.

**Acquisition loop.** Mandatory pre-departure orientation is a genuine congregation point. Returnee word of mouth. Cooperative lenders who want to know if the loan is against a real job.

**Monetization.** This is the problem, and I am not going to disguise it. Candidates: (a) the worker pays — morally grotesque, charging a debt-bonded person for protection; (b) the agency pays for a trust badge — regulatory capture by the abuser; (c) the destination employer or brand pays for ethical-recruitment assurance — real budgets exist post-Qatar-World-Cup, but that is an ESG enterprise sale from Kathmandu; (d) the cooperative lender pays for loan-underwriting verification — the most honest option and the smallest market.

**Alternatives today.** eMigrate, FWCMS, pre-departure orientation, Facebook returnee groups, and the broker's word.

**Unfair-advantage hypothesis.** A returnee-sourced dataset of what each agency and each employer actually paid versus promised.

**Fatal risks.** (1) **The fee is paid before the worker would ever open the app** — the same structural failure that neutered pre-departure orientation centres, documented explicitly. (2) State platforms in this space *"often function as state-sanctioned monopolies that digitize existing exploitative structures."* (3) The distribution channel is the abuser. (4) No payer who is not compromised. (5) Getting it wrong gets someone hurt.

**Evidence that would raise confidence:** a Gulf employer or a Nepali cooperative federation putting money on the table. **Evidence that would kill it:** confirmation that fee payment precedes any digital touchpoint — which is what the record already says.

## 3.5 Dossier — C4 "OWNPIPE"
### Demand independence for US home-services contractors

**Problem narrative.** Angi/HomeAdvisor charges $15–$125 per lead shared with three to six contractors, plus $300–$500 annual membership, plus a 35% cancellation fee on the remaining contract term; one aggregator counted 2,064 BBB complaints in three years, and contractors report 20–40% of leads are ghosts or fabricated. Thumbtack charges $40–$75 the moment you respond, and roughly 75% of "direct leads" never reply. The escape route everyone recommends — own website, Google Business Profile, Local Services Ads — requires marketing skills a two-truck plumber does not have.

**Specific user portrait.** Marcus, 38, two trucks, four employees, residential HVAC in Mesa, Arizona. Spent $14,000 with Angi last year and closed maybe eleven jobs. Has a Facebook page and a phone number. His wife answers the phone between school runs, and calls missed between 9am and 4pm are simply lost.

**Job to be done.** *"Get me booked jobs I don't have to buy from Angi."*

**Beachhead.** HVAC, plumbing and electrical, 1–3 trucks, in Phoenix and Dallas metros.

**Acquisition loop.** r/HVAC, r/sweatystartup, ContractorTalk, Facebook groups ("Painting Contractors USA", "The Painter's Roundtable"), supply-house counters (Ferguson, SiteOne), and search terms like "Angi alternative" that already have measurable volume.

**Monetization.** $99–$249/month. Real budget, currently being handed to Angi.

**Alternatives today.** Angi, Thumbtack, Google LSA, Jobber, Housecall Pro, QuoteIQ, and a nephew who does websites.

**Unfair-advantage hypothesis.** Being the anti-Angi with an unarguable receipt: cost per booked job, shown weekly.

**Fatal risks.** (1) This is a marketing agency wearing SaaS clothes — the work is Google Business Profile management, review solicitation and call answering, and that is people, not software. (2) Jobber and Housecall Pro are well capitalised and adjacent. (3) CAC is paid-search-shaped, which the brief penalises. (4) Google LSA is already the free-market answer and it works. (5) No structural pull — Marcus can keep suffering indefinitely, and does.

**Evidence that would raise confidence:** a cohort of contractors cancelling Angi and attributing it. **Evidence that would kill it:** the honest observation that the winning answer is "hire a marketing person," which is not a $20,000 app.

## 3.6 Dossier — C5 "HOLDFAST"
### Neutral custody and proof for Lagos rent deposits and tenancy

**Problem narrative.** Lagos law caps agency fees at 10% of annual rent and LASRERA has declared caution, inspection and finder's fees unlawful. Reality: a tenant quoted by name paid 37.5% agency plus 37.5% legal on ₦400,000 rent, plus ₦100,000 "refundable" damages deposit and ₦15,000 in viewing fees, to an agent registered with nobody. LASRERA accreditation became compulsory in June 2025 and tenancy-agreement registration is mandatory. The "refundable" deposit is refundable in the same sense that a promise is a contract.

**Specific user portrait.** Tolu, 29, first Lagos flat, works at a bank in Victoria Island, moving to Yaba. Has ₦1.4m saved, will hand roughly ₦400,000 of it to strangers in cash or transfer against a Word document, and has no idea whether the man with the keys owns the building.

**Job to be done.** *"Don't let me lose ₦400,000 to a man I met last Tuesday, and give me something I can show if this goes wrong."*

**Beachhead.** Yaba, Surulere and Lekki Phase 1 renters aged 24–35 in salaried employment, reached through employer HR at banks and telcos.

**Acquisition loop.** Employer HR relocation packs; LASRERA as a legitimacy partner; the tenancy-registration obligation as a hook; and a forwardable custody receipt that circulates in the WhatsApp groups where Lagos house-hunting actually happens.

**Monetization.** 1–2% custody fee on the deposit, plus float, plus a fee for a LASRERA-registered tenancy agreement.

**Alternatives today.** Cash and prayer. Jiji and PropertyPro listings. A lawyer at ₦50k. Asking your uncle.

**Unfair-advantage hypothesis.** Regulatory alignment — being the deposit scheme LASRERA points at, the way the UK's TDS and DPS became statutory infrastructure and now hold deposits by default.

**Fatal risks.** (1) Holding other people's money in Nigeria means CBN licensing, which is not a $20,000 project. (2) Landlords have no reason to accept custody and every reason to prefer cash today. (3) The agent is the fraud and the agent controls the introduction. (4) One-transaction-a-year frequency — no habit, no retention. (5) Deposit disputes require adjudication, which is an operations business with a legal team, not an app.

**Evidence that would raise confidence:** LASRERA publicly endorsing a custody scheme, or one large employer mandating it. **Evidence that would kill it:** landlords refusing custody at any meaningful rate — and the fee structure evidence suggests they will.

## 3.7 Kill list — strong rejects that did not make the Five

| Rejected | Score | Why it lost to the Five |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghana E-VAT for ex-flat-rate traders** | 7 | Genuinely strong, and the closest thing to a sixth candidate. Lost because GRA supplies free invoicing software, GUTA already negotiated a Q1 2026 reprieve, and the ₦-equivalent ARPU is lower than Nigeria's while the buyer-coercion mechanism is weaker (Ghana's chain pressure is less documented). **Retained as the first expansion market**, not as a candidate. |
| **Kenya buyer-initiated invoicing** | 8 | The mechanism is superb and it is the intellectual source of C2. Lost as a *beachhead* because Kenya is three years into eTIMS, KRA built its own manual escape hatch, and the whitespace is narrower than Nigeria's. **Retained as expansion market #2**, where the same product needs less re-education. |
| **Nigerian invoice factoring / supply-chain finance** | 7 | The Factoring Regulation Bill is still a bill. The brief forbids depending on a law that has not passed. Also: CycleFlow launched with IFC backing, so the well-capitalised version already exists. **Retained as the year-two monetisation layer for C2**, where an IRN-validated invoice is a natively financeable asset. |
| **Nigerian last-mile paperwork (TIN/CAC/NIN)** | 5 | Kippa Start ran this and shut it down. One-off transaction, no retention, agent is the competitor. |
| **UK Making Tax Digital for sole traders** | 5 | HMRC publishes a list of free compatible software. Competing with a government-blessed free tier for a user who already has an accountant. |
| **Poland KSeF for micro-firms** | 6 | Strongest European forced demand, but the accountant owns the client relationship and will not be disintermediated by a phone app. |
| **Verified rental listings (as opposed to deposit custody)** | 5 | Adversarial supply side. C5 is the only survivable shape of this problem and even it is fragile. |
| **Medicine authentication** | 3 | Seventeen years of evidence that the frightened person will not verify and nobody else will pay. Killed in Phase 2, and I stand behind it. |
| **Nanostore restocking** | 3 | Copia, Twiga, MarketForce. The graveyard is the argument. |
