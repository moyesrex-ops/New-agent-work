# Phase 7 — Naming

## 7.1 What the name has to survive

Not a mood board. Six specific tests drawn from the simulations:

1. **The Oshodi test.** A fabricator hears it once, spoken across a workshop with a generator running, and repeats it correctly to his brother that evening.
2. **The procurement test.** It appears on a purchase requisition — *"₦6,000,000 per annum, supplier compliance platform"* — and a Nigerian Financial Controller signs without wincing.
3. **The scam test.** A supplier receives a WhatsApp link from a name he has never heard, and the name itself lowers rather than raises his suspicion. **This is the most important test**, because SIM 4 identified "is this a scam?" as the primary first-run obstacle.
4. **The document test.** It sits in the footer of a tax document that a NRS auditor may read.
5. **The collision test.** No famous app, no local slang, no adjacent-sector incumbent, no insult.
6. **The 16px test.** It fits under an app icon on a cheap Android launcher without truncating.

One correction to my own instincts before starting: I initially assumed the supplier-facing name needed *warmth* and the buyer-facing name needed *gravity*, and that this justified two names. Re-reading SIM 4 and SIM 8, that is wrong. The supplier's dominant emotion at first contact is **suspicion**, not coldness. What reduces suspicion in Nigerian micro-commerce is not friendliness — it is **officialdom**. Shop signs say *Federal*, *National*, *Trust*, *Union* for a reason. So both audiences want the same thing from the name, and **one name serves both**. That also saves a second wordmark on a $20,000 budget.

## 7.2 Eight candidates and honest kill notes

| # | Candidate | Origin / logic | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sabi** | Nigerian Pidgin, "to know / know-how." Maximum stickiness. | **KILL — collision.** Sabi is a large, well-funded Nigerian B2B commerce platform. Not arguable. |
| 2 | **Kola** | The kola nut is the West African object that *seals an agreement* — Igbo `iwa ojî` is literally the ritual that validates a transaction. Semantically perfect. | **KILL — slang.** "Kola" is also Nigerian slang for a **bribe** ("give am kola"). Naming an anti-leakage tax-compliance product after a bribe would be the single funniest mistake in this memo. This is exactly why the brief demands a local-slang check. |
| 3 | **Erí** | Yoruba `ẹ̀rí` = proof, evidence, testimony `OBSERVED` (YorubaNames; Wiktionary). The literal name of what the product produces. Genuinely the most beautiful option. | **KILL — sector collision + tonal ambiguity.** ERI is a Swiss core-banking software company (OLYMPIC Banking System, 169 staff, 16k LinkedIn followers) — same trademark classes, same buyer persona, and it would poison search results permanently. Separately, `ẹri` with mid tones means *river*, so the written form is ambiguous without diacritics, and diacritics break domains, email and WhatsApp display names. |
| 4 | **Gbam** | Nigerian Pidgin/Igbo interjection meaning *exactly / correct / case closed* — described in the literature as "a verbal stamp of approval" `OBSERVED`. One syllable, `gb` plosive, unbeatable in market noise. | **KILL — collision, and the worst possible one.** Gbam is a **licensed Nigerian lottery and gaming company** operating at `gbam.ng` since 2016, with national press coverage in ThisDay, Guardian NG and The Nation. The brief's own confusion test lists *casino* as a failure mode. A tax-compliance brand that shares a name with a scratch-card lottery is dead on arrival with a Financial Controller. |
| 5 | **Confam** | Pidgin for *verified / genuine*. "Na confam." | **KILL — overclaim.** The ethics constraints state plainly that we are never the source of truth; the NRS is. A name meaning "we verify it" contradicts the product's central honesty. Also reads as slang on a contract. |
| 6 | **Shaida** | Hausa for *witness / proof*. Strong consonants, excellent in noise, nationally legible. | **KILL — religious adjacency.** Arabic-derived from the same root as *shahada*, the Islamic declaration of faith. In a country this religiously balanced, that is an unforced error in either direction. |
| 7 | **Risiti** | Nigerian Pidgin phonetic form of *receipt*. Warm, instantly understood, three syllables, hard consonants. | **KILL — semantically wrong, and wrong register.** We produce compliant **invoices**, not receipts, and in a tax context that distinction matters to the exact person signing the cheque. Held longest of the rejects. |
| 8 | **Stampa** | The Nigerian Pidgin phonological form of *stamper*. Pidgin naturalises English words to an open `-a` ending as a rule: bumper→**bumpa**, keeper→**kippa**, better→**beta**, water→**wata**, brother→**broda**. Two successful Nigerian B2B brands — Bumpa and Kippa — are built on exactly this construction. | **SELECTED.** |

Also considered and dropped without a full entry: **Tally** (Tally is the dominant Indian accounting incumbent — fatal in this category), **Reckon** (ASX-listed Australian accounting software), **Vendra** (Latinate, cold, could belong to any SaaS on earth — fails the Oshodi test), **Vouch** (US insurtech), **Paysafe** (global payments giant), **Ezi** (phonetically collides with Eazipay, a Nigerian payroll/invoicing company).

## 7.3 The name

# STAMPA

**Company:** Stampa Technologies Limited (Nigeria, CAC-registered).
**Product / app name:** Stampa. Same word. No sub-brands.
**Buyer console:** Stampa Console — a surface, not a second brand.
**Free acquisition wedge:** the Stampa Exposure Report.
**The proof object:** *the stamp* (lowercase). "Send me the stamp." Deliberately genericised, because we want that to become the word.

### Why it wins the six tests

| Test | How it passes |
|---|---|
| **Oshodi** | Two syllables, STAM-pa. Open `-a` ending matches how Nigerian Pidgin already reshapes English words, so it does not feel like a foreign word being learned — it feels like a word that already existed. |
| **Procurement** | "Stampa Technologies Limited" reads as a company. Same register as Moniepoint, Interswitch, Duplo — all of whom sell to Nigerian enterprise finance teams today. |
| **Scam** | It names an **official act**. A document that has been stamped is, in Nigerian commercial culture, a document that is real. The name itself argues for legitimacy before a single screen loads. |
| **Document** | Short, sober, no slang, no exclamation. Sits in a footer without embarrassment. |
| **Collision** | No Nigerian company found in the compliance, invoicing, accounting or fintech space. *La Stampa* is an Italian newspaper and "stampa" is Italian for *printing* — neither operates in this market or this class. Cleanest of the eight. |
| **16px** | Six characters. No truncation on any launcher. |

### The one honest weakness

The `/st/` onset is a fricative-plus-stop cluster, which is measurably weaker in noise than a plosive onset like Kippa's `/k/` or Bumpa's `/b/`. Across a loud workshop it could be misheard as "Tampa" or "Stanna."

**Mitigations, and they are real rather than decorative:**
- The `-AMPA` nucleus is loud — open vowel, nasal, plosive — so the *back* of the word carries when the front is masked.
- Recognition is visual as well as verbal: the mark is a stamp impression, so a supplier who mishears the name still recognises the icon on the WhatsApp message.
- Nigerian financial branding is full of well-understood `/st/` onsets — Stanbic IBTC, Sterling Bank, Standard Chartered. This is a known shape, not a foreign one.
- The name is always spoken with the tagline attached in the first ten seconds of any introduction.

### Taglines

Three written, one chosen.

1. **"Get it stamped. Get paid."** ← **CHOSEN**
2. "The stamp your customer is waiting for."
3. "Every invoice, official."

**Why #1.** It is the supplier's entire story in five words, in the imperative rhythm Nigerian commercial speech actually uses. It names the action *and* the reward, which is the only reason he will finish the flow. It fits a receipt footer, a store subtitle and a WhatsApp bio. And it makes a promise the product can actually keep — nothing about "grow your business," nothing about "digital transformation."

**Buyer-facing variant** (used only on the console and in enterprise material, same voice): *"Bring the whole supply chain in."*

### Domains and handles

| Surface | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Primary domain | `stampa.ng` — the `.ng` is a deliberate trust asset in this market, not a compromise |
| Fallback / international | `getstampa.com`, `stampa.africa` |
| App links | `stampa.ng/s/<code>` — short enough to survive a WhatsApp preview |
| Email | `hello@stampa.ng`, `support@stampa.ng`, `<firstname>@stampa.ng` |
| Social | `@stampa_ng` on X, Instagram, LinkedIn |
| WhatsApp Business display name | **Stampa** — with the Official Business Account tick, which is a P0 requirement, not a nice-to-have |
| App store name | **Stampa: Compliant Invoices** (title), keyword logic in Phase 22 |

### Pronunciation

**STAM-pa.** /ˈstam.pa/. First syllable stressed, second syllable an open `a` as in *father*, never a schwa. Not "Stam-per," not "Stam-pah-ah."

Written without diacritics everywhere, always. There is no accented variant, no stylised lowercase-only rule, no alternate spelling. One spelling, one pronunciation, one word.
