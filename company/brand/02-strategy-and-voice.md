# Phase 8 — Brand Strategy and Voice

## 8.1 Brand operating system

### Who it is for

Two people who will never meet, who need the same document, and who currently blame each other for not having it.

**Ify** — Tax Manager, ₦40bn FMCG manufacturer, Agbara. Competent, under-resourced, accountable to a Financial Controller who wants a number by Friday. She is not impressed by software. She is impressed by *not having to explain herself in an audit*.

**Emeka** — six-person aluminium fabrication workshop, Oshodi. Owns the business, does the quoting, drives the van. His question is never "is this a good product." It is **"is this real, and will it get me my money."**

The brand's job is to be the same object to both of them: **the stamp that makes the paper real**.

### Who it must never feel like

| Must never feel like | Why it would kill us |
|---|---|
| **A tax collector** | Emeka's central fear is that this app is the government reaching into his business. If Stampa reads as an arm of the NRS, adoption dies at the door. We work *for* the supplier, alongside the buyer, in front of the regulator. |
| **A loan app** | Nigerian digital lending has poisoned an entire aesthetic — urgent green gradients, countdown timers, "get ₦50,000 now." Any visual overlap with that category is a trust catastrophe, and the harassment record is why. |
| **A lottery or betting brand** | Bright, loud, exclamatory, scratch-card energy. We killed a name for this exact reason. |
| **A generic global SaaS** | A gradient, a teal circle-arrow, an isometric illustration of a person at a laptop. Ify has seen forty of these and remembers none. Emeka would not believe it is for him. |
| **An NGO or a donor programme** | Warm photography of smiling traders, hands on shoulders, "empowering MSMEs." Emeka is not a beneficiary. He is a supplier with an unpaid invoice. |

### Promise

**Your invoice becomes official, and your customer can pay it.**

That is the whole promise. Not growth, not insights, not digital transformation. It is small, checkable, and it is the only thing either user asked for.

### Proof

Not adjectives. Four mechanisms:

1. **The IRN itself.** A real government reference number returned in seconds. We did not issue it, and we say so.
2. **The QR that verifies against the NRS, not against us.** Anyone can check the document without trusting Stampa. A brand that hands you the means to doubt it is a brand you end up trusting.
3. **Bank details we cannot edit.** Stated in the interface, at the moment it matters.
4. **A stamp you can forward.** The proof is a portable object the supplier owns, not a login he has to keep.

### Personality — exactly five words

**Official. Plain. Quick. Steady. On your side.**

*Official* is first because it is the trust mechanism. *On your side* is last because it is the tiebreaker whenever officialdom and warmth conflict — and it is the line that separates us from the tax authority.

### The enemy — the old way

**Silence.** Not a competitor. The enemy is the three weeks after delivery when nothing happens, nobody explains why, and a six-person payroll comes due. On the buyer's side, the enemy is the same silence in a different costume: a spreadsheet of 612 vendors with no idea which of them can issue a valid invoice.

Everything the brand does is anti-silence. Status is always visible. Failures are explained in words with a next step. Nothing is ever "pending" without a reason and a time.

### Trust sources

**Primary: officialness.** The stamp, the reference number, the QR, the document that looks like a document. This is doing the heavy lifting in a market where the first question is "is this a scam."

**Secondary: competence.** Fast, correct, no clutter, no marketing inside the product. Ify buys competence; Emeka recognises it.

**Tertiary: severity — used sparingly and never as a sales tactic.** The deadline is real and we will state it once, plainly. We will not run countdown timers, red banners, or "act now or lose ₦52m." Fear-selling a compliance product to a micro-supplier is the behaviour of the scammers we are warning him about.

**Deliberately not used: warmth as a primary source.** No mascots, no first names, no exclamation marks, no emoji in transactional surfaces. Warmth appears only in support and in failure — the two places it is earned rather than performed.

### Cultural notes for Lagos

Learned from the market, not from a style guide.

- **What looks rich:** restraint, generous white space, real photography of real documents, heavyweight paper stock in print, deep saturated single colours rather than gradients, and *precision* — aligned edges, consistent corners, no wobble.
- **What looks scammy:** gradients, glossy 3D buttons, urgency copy, unexplained numbers, stock photos of white people in suits, any use of the word "instant" next to money, WhatsApp messages with more than one link, and shortened URLs.
- **What looks governmental (and why we want *some* of it):** green and white, a coat-of-arms-adjacent seal, serif type, a reference number in monospace, formal register. We want the *document* to borrow this. We do not want the *brand* to be mistaken for it. The line: **our documents may look official; our app must never look like a government portal**, because government portals in this market are associated with failure and queueing.
- **What looks childish:** rounded everything, illustration-led onboarding, pastel palettes, cartoon empty states. Emeka runs a business with a payroll. He is not being onboarded to a game.
- **What looks extractive:** anything that gates the supplier. Any upsell in the supplier app. Any moment where the small user is asked for money. This is a brand rule and an ethics rule and they are the same rule.

### The competitive glance test

Cover the logo. Could this be a random fintech, health app or marketplace?

No, and here is the mechanical reason: **the brand's central object is a document, not a screen.** Stampa's most-seen surface is not the app — it is a stamped invoice sitting in a WhatsApp thread. The identity is built for ink, paper, monochrome, QR codes, and a 400×300 preview card, and it is designed backwards from those constraints. A fintech brand is built for a card and a balance. A marketplace brand is built for a grid of things. Ours is built for a **seal on paper**, which is why it cannot be swapped with either.

## 8.2 Voice charter

### Reading level

**Grade 6 English**, and every sentence must survive translation into Nigerian Pidgin without losing meaning. Test: if a sentence cannot be said out loud in Pidgin by a support agent on the phone, rewrite it.

Sentences under 12 words in the app. Under 20 in the console. No sentence contains two ideas.

### Words we use

`stamp` · `stamped` · `invoice` · `customer` · `paid` · `sent` · `check` · `fix` · `try again` · `we will` · `you can` · `now` · `today` · `reference number` · `your customer` · `free`

Always **"your customer"**, never "the buyer" or "the counterparty," when speaking to a supplier. The relationship is his, not ours.

### Words we ban

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| `seamless`, `robust`, `leverage`, `empower`, `unlock`, `revolutionise`, `solution` | Consultant noise. Ify has read a hundred of these and believes none. |
| `simply`, `just`, `easy` | If it were simple he would have done it. Telling a struggling user it is easy is an insult with a smile. |
| `oops`, `uh oh`, `whoops` | Something went wrong with a man's ₦1.8m invoice. It is not cute. |
| `instant`, `guaranteed`, `100%` | Scam vocabulary in this market. Also we cannot guarantee the NRS. |
| `verified by Stampa` | We do not verify. The NRS does. **This is an ethics line, not a style preference.** |
| `MSME`, `SME`, `informal sector`, `the unbanked` | Development-sector words. Emeka does not think of himself as a segment. |
| `!` in any transactional message | Reserved for nothing. There is no approved use. |
| Emoji in any invoice, receipt, status or error surface | Allowed only in the WhatsApp support persona, and only in reply. |

### How we talk when something fails

Three parts, in this order, always: **what happened → why → what to do next.** Never two of the three. Never an apology without a next step.

> **Not stamped.**
> The NRS did not accept the customer TIN: 1234567-0001.
> Ask your customer to confirm the number, then tap Try again. Your invoice is saved.

Note what is doing the work: the failure is named plainly, blame is placed on a *fact* rather than on the user, the exact bad value is shown, the next action is one tap, and the last sentence removes the fear of having lost the work. That last sentence is the most important one in the product.

### How we talk when money or documents move

Slower, fuller sentences. Exact figures with currency. Name the counterparty. Show the reference. Never use relative time ("recently," "a moment ago") — use a timestamp.

> **Stamped.**
> Invoice INV-0032 to Dangote Cement Plc, ₦1,850,000.00 including ₦129,000.00 VAT.
> NRS reference IRN-7K2M-88QX-2026. Stamped 14 Sep 2026, 10:42 WAT.
> Send the stamp to your customer.

### Example lines

| Moment | Line |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Stamped. Send it to your customer. |
| **Waiting** | Sending to NRS. This takes about 20 seconds. |
| **Slow network** | Still trying. Your invoice is saved — you can close the app and we will finish. |
| **Offline** | No network. We saved this and will send it when you are back online. |
| **Error (ours)** | We could not reach the NRS. Nothing was lost. We are trying again — no action needed from you. |
| **Error (theirs)** | The NRS rejected this: VAT total does not match the line items. Check the amounts, then Try again. |
| **Help** | Call us on 0700-STAMPA, 8am–8pm. Or send a WhatsApp message and we will reply. |
| **Permission** | We need your camera to scan your customer's code. Nothing is uploaded until you press Send. |
| **Payment (buyer console only)** | Your plan covers 250 active suppliers. You have 212. Next invoice 1 Oct 2026, ₦424,000.00. |
| **Anti-scam, shown on first open and monthly** | Stampa is free for suppliers. Nobody should ever ask you to pay to register. If someone does, call us on 0700-STAMPA. |

### Support macros, same voice

**Opening:** *"Stampa here. Tell me the invoice number and I will check it now."*

**Diagnosis:** *"I can see it. The NRS rejected it because the customer TIN has one digit missing. I have fixed the number on our side — try sending it again and tell me what happens."*

**Cannot fix today:** *"This one is with the NRS, not with us. I have logged it as case 4471. I will message you by 4pm today with an update, even if there is no news yet."*

**The scam report:** *"You were right to call. Stampa never charges suppliers and no Stampa staff will ever ask you for money or for your bank password. Send me the person's number and I will report it. Your account is safe."*

**Closing:** *"Anything else on this invoice? If not, I will close case 4471. Call back any time."*

Rules for support: reply in the language the message arrived in — English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa. Never end a conversation without either a resolution or a **named time** for the next update. Never say "please bear with us."
