# Phase 2 — Longlist

54 opportunities collected. Provisional score is a gut 1–10 on **"would users come without a miracle"** — that is the only question at this stage. Scores here are provisional and deliberately harsh; the full 15-dimension rubric is applied in Phases 4–5 to the Five only.

Bucket key: **A** = a startup is already pulling users here, study why. **B** = painful demand, weak or absent product. **C** = transferable model awaiting re-localisation.

## 2.2 Repeating arrival mechanisms, now with counts

Of the 54, the number where I could name a *specific, dated, non-advertising* reason a user arrives this quarter:

| Mechanism | Count | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| A counterparty withholds money until you comply | **8** | Strongest. Commercial, not political. |
| A dated legal deadline with a penalty | 11 | Strong but politically reversible (see Malaysia) |
| An organised trade association acts as channel | 4 | Strong, underused |
| Rage at an extractive incumbent | 3 | Real but needs a cheaper channel to receive it |
| Agent networks | 3 | Works, costs money |
| Fear (fraud, death, theft) | 9 | **Loud but does not convert.** Fear produces reading, not paying |
| Convenience / better product | 16 | Weakest. This is the Kippa graveyard |

**`INFERRED`:** the fear cluster is the trap. Counterfeit medicine, safety apps, rental fraud and impersonation all produce enormous engagement with the *article* about the problem and near-zero willingness to pay for the *product*. Seventeen years of mPedigree and Sproxil is the proof. I will not shortlist a fear product without a named payer who is not the frightened person.

## 2.3 Killed in public

Fashionable and unforced ideas, named and buried.

| Killed | Why it dies |
|---|---|
| **AI bookkeeping copilot for African SMEs** | AI as perfume. Kippa's post-mortem: *"need did not convert to revenue."* Adding an LLM does not create a payer. |
| **AI tutor for out-of-school children** | Explicitly forbidden by the brief, and correctly — the payer is a donor, not a user, and donor-funded products do not have retention. |
| **Blockchain land registry for Lagos** | The problem is a corrupt registry, not an immutable one. Web3 solving a governance problem is a category error. |
| **Verified-listings rental marketplace (Lagos)** | Score 5–6. The supply side you must recruit *is the fraud*. Two-sided cold start with an adversarial supply side is the worst possible starting position. Survives as a limited **Candidate 5** in a deposit-custody form only. |
| **Medicine authentication scanner** | Score 3. mPedigree (2007) and Sproxil have run this experiment for seventeen years. NAFDAC's own MAS scheme has *"low utilisation"*. The patient will not scan and the pharmacy will not pay. Loud, unfundable. |
| **Nanostore restocking (Wasoko-for-X)** | Copia, Twiga and MarketForce all collapsed or shrank. Negative gross margin dressed as a network effect. |
| **EUDR cocoa traceability SaaS** | Fairtrade already offers Satelligence deforestation risk assessment **free** to producer organisations. Competing with free, against an enterprise buying cycle, for a smallholder user. |
| **Gig-worker super-app** | Platform on day one. Banned. |
| **Another POS/inventory app for Lagos market traders** | MyTreda already runs per-market landing pages for Balogun, Alaba, Computer Village, Ladipo, Ariaria and Onitsha, and Moniepoint bundles Moniebook at ₦6,000/mo against a payment relationship it already owns. Nothing to add. |
| **Ivorian FNE mobile app** | The DGI shipped one. Competing with the regulator's free first-party app on the regulator's own mandate is a bad trade. |
| **Malaysian MyInvois for micro-SMEs** | Mandate withdrawn 1 Sept 2026. 1.1m businesses exempted. A market that ceased to exist yesterday. |
| **Migrant recruitment-fee transparency app** | Advances to **Candidate 3** and is killed there, properly, with the simulation shown. I am not burying the most morally serious idea in a table. |

## 2.4 What survives into Phase 3

Highest-scoring survivors: **#1 + #4** (the same market seen from two sides — supplier and buyer), **#9** (Kenya buyer-initiated invoicing), **#2** (Ghana), **#43** (US contractors), **#15** (Nigerian payment cycles), **#20/#22** (Lagos housing), **#28** (migrant debt), **#41/#42** (last-mile paperwork).

Entries **#1 and #4 are not two ideas.** They are the supplier side and the buyer side of one market, and which side you sell to is the entire strategic question. Phase 3 splits them deliberately into two of the Five so the simulations can decide, rather than me deciding by assertion.
