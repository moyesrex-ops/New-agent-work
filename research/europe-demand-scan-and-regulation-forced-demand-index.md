## 0. The single most important fact in this memo

`OBSERVED` **France's B2B e-invoicing mandate goes live tomorrow — 1 September 2026.** The rulebook was completed only on 28 July 2026 (Decree No. 2026-677 + Order of 27 July 2026, JORF No. 174, in force 29 July) — **five weeks before go-live** — and on 11 July, Minister David Amiel (press release No. 898) confirmed no postponement while promising a "tolerant and benevolent" posture toward good-faith businesses. Full scope in the table below. `INFERRED` A five-week rulebook gap plus an explicitly tolerant first phase is the signature of a mandate that will be *technically* met and *operationally* broken — the conditions under which a new entrant wins on "just make it work" rather than on features.

`OBSERVED` Poland already ran the experiment. On KSeF's first mandatory day (1 Feb 2026), with only ~320,000 users, the production API buckled: mass errors, and the *urzędowe potwierdzenie odbioru* (UPO, which assigns the KSeF number) took **over five hours** instead of seconds. Firms that cannot release warehouse goods without a KSeF number froze. Correction invoices require the original's KSeF number — which many issuers did not yet have — so complaint and settlement processes blocked too. Sources: [pitax.pl](https://www.pitax.pl/wiedza/aktualnosci/ruszyl-obowiazkowy-ksef-jakie-problemy-spotkaly-firmy/), [itwiz.pl](https://itwiz.pl/czy_ksef-wytrzyma-naplyw-milionow-firm/).

Format: **problem → who hurts → today's workaround → why they show up → analog → death risk.**

**1. Abschlagsrechnung-native e-invoicing for German trades.**
Progress invoices, retentions and Bauabzugsteuer as first-class objects, XRechnung/ZUGFeRD from the cheapest tier. Hurts: German Handwerksbetriebe above €800k, mandated to issue structured invoices **1 Jan 2027** on their 2026 books. Workaround: Lexware XL at €32.90/month, or Word plus a PDF printer. Show up because: a hard date and a real cash impact — the customer's input VAT hangs on your format. Analog: Fattura24's rise on Italy's SdI mandate. Death risk: DATEV or Lexware simply move the feature down a tier; the €800k cohort is small; 2028 universality lets everyone wait.

**2. MTD filing for the 75% who haven't signed up.**
£4–8/month, spreadsheet-and-photo in, four quarterly updates plus Final Declaration out, no chart of accounts, no onboarding call. Hurts: ~650,000 UK sole traders and landlords in scope since 6 Apr 2026 and not yet enrolled; 90% never used cloud accounting. Workaround: nothing, or an accountant who cannot serve them below cost. Show up because: penalty points begin biting after the 2026-27 holiday, and quarterly updates gate the tax return itself. Analog: TaxScouts, Coconut. Death risk: HMRC ships a free tool; Xero/QuickBooks drop a £5 tier; a threshold delay to 2028.

**3. Landlord-specific MTD, jointly-let-property first.**
Per-property P&L, jointly-let apportionment (which HMRC treats specially in quarterly updates), agent statement ingestion. Hurts: UK landlords above £50k now, £30k from Apr 2027, £20k from Apr 2028. Workaround: a spreadsheet plus an accountant who does not understand letting-agent statements. Show up: the £30k tranche is the mass market and it is 7 months out. Analog: Hammock, Landlord Studio. Death risk: crowded; letting-agent statement formats are a permanent integration tax.

**4. Deposit escrow with land-registry-verified landlord identity.**
Money held until keys turn in the lock; ownership verified against Grundbuch / Kadaster / RTB register; reverse image search on listing photos at ingest. Hurts: renters and students in Berlin (4,862 fraud cases, +32%), Dublin (>€400k lost by July 2026), Amsterdam (€2,000–€5,000 typical loss). Workaround: reverse image search and hope; 82% never report, 1% recover. Show up: it is the only moment in the transaction where trust is worth paying for, and platforms have no liability. Analog: Escrow.com, Reposit, Flatfair. Death risk: payments licensing and AML in every market; portals ship "verified landlord" badges free; two-sided cold start.

**5. Portable trade reputation.**
An issuer-signed, exportable record of completed jobs, photos, warranties and verified reviews that the tradesperson owns and can attest from an EUDI wallet as a Qualified Electronic Attestation of Attributes. Hurts: UK and DACH tradespeople locked into 12-month directory contracts whose *"reviews are not portable."* Workaround: screenshot the profile before it comes down and beg customers for fresh Google reviews. Show up at renewal, when the retention agent calls. Analog: credit-file portability, Open Banking. Death risk: reputation without demand is worthless — this only works bundled with lead flow; wallet acceptance slips past 2027; directories litigate.

**6. Posted-worker compliance pack as a product.**
One record per worker per jurisdiction: A1 application filed before mobilisation, Meldeportal entry, wage attestation, SOKA-BAU allocation, timesheets — exportable in 5 working days on demand. Hurts: general contractors in DE/FR/AT/NL running Polish, Czech, Romanian, Hungarian and Portuguese subcontract labour, facing €1,000–€10,000 per worker, 25–45% of gross clawback, joint-and-several liability, and from 1 Jan 2026 near-immediate criminal referral in Germany. Workaround: email threads and a shared drive assembled retroactively at audit. Show up: the first FKS inspection. Analog: Deel/Remote for EOR; Onfido for document verification. Death risk: enterprise sales cycle, low frequency; EESSI plus the proposed EU e-declaration portal could commoditise filing; incumbent payroll adds a module.

**7. Live-in care hours ledger.**
Phone-based, multilingual, offline-capable logging that distinguishes *Vollarbeit* from *Bereitschaftsdienst* from free time, timestamped and tamper-evident, held by the worker and shareable with family, agency and court. Hurts: ~hundreds of thousands of live-in carers in German and Austrian households, plus the families carrying undeclared-work and back-pay risk on €2,200–€3,500/month arrangements. Workaround: nothing — which is precisely why the BAG had to remand a case to establish the hours. Show up: at the dispute, or when the family's lawyer asks. Analog: Deputy, ECM in UK home care. Death risk: neither party wants an audit trail; who pays is genuinely unclear; agencies actively hostile.

**8. Ukrainian status-transition case management.**
Guided conversion from temporary protection into employment-, study- or family-based national residence, per country, with document checklists, deadlines and qualification-recognition routing. Hurts: **4.38m beneficiaries** whose protection now runs to 4 Mar 2028 while the Council pushes Member States to "step up" transition; plus new arrivals facing the military-obligation evidence gate from 5 Aug 2026. Workaround: Telegram groups, volunteer lawyers, NGO caseworkers. Show up: statuses expire and employers demand proof. Analog: Boundless, Immigo, Jobbatical. Death risk: end-users cannot pay; the buyer is a fragmented set of NGOs and municipalities; the war ending changes everything.

**9. Small-buyer AP: reject-proof invoice intake.**
For the micro-business *receiving* mandated e-invoices: pull from KSeF / SdI / Peppol / e-Factura, reconcile to bank, flag anything that endangers input VAT, and generate the corrective document (Italy's TD29, a KSeF *faktura korygująca*). Hurts: every Polish micro-firm receiving KSeF invoices since 1 Feb 2026; every Belgian VAT payer since 1 Jan 2026; every Italian business; every French business from tomorrow. Workaround: log into the government portal, download, re-key — and in Poland accept that you cannot mark anything as paid there. Show up: an accountant asking "where is the invoice?", or blocked input VAT. Analog: Dext, Candis, Fitek. Death risk: the tax authority's own client improves; PA/KSeF providers bundle it free; deep per-country integration cost.

**10. Cross-border invoice-format translation for the September 2026 cohort.**
A French SME must *receive* structured invoices from tomorrow but need not *issue* until Sep 2027; its Belgian supplier must send Peppol BIS; its Italian one goes through SdI; its German one is on ZUGFeRD; its Polish one on FA(3). Sell a single endpoint that normalises in both directions. Hurts: SMEs trading across two or more mandate regimes. Workaround: one PA/PDP contract per country, or paper. Show up: an invoice gets rejected and a customer refuses to pay. Analog: Storecove, Tickstar, Unifiedpost. Death risk: this is exactly what ViDA's 2030 DRR and Peppol are designed to make unnecessary; incumbent access points are already here.

**11. Pay-transparency compliance for the 100–250 employee band.**
Job architecture, "work of equal value" categorisation, pay-range publication for ads, right-to-information response workflow, and the ≥5% joint-pay-assessment trigger — priced for a 120-person firm, not a multinational. Hurts: mid-market employers in 23 Member States that missed the 7 Jun 2026 transposition deadline and now face first reports on **7 Jun 2027 using calendar-2026 data being generated right now**. Workaround: a consultant and a spreadsheet. Show up: the data window is closing and the compensation cycle is live. Analog: Ravio, Figures, Trusaic. Death risk: incumbent HRIS ships it; per-country transposition divergence (France/Denmark at 50 employees, Netherlands including agency workers) multiplies build cost.

**12. Platform-work algorithmic-management audit and human-in-the-loop layer.**
Decision logging, human review queues for suspensions and terminations, DPIA artefacts, prohibited-data-processing controls (no emotional-state inference, no predictive union-activity targeting). Hurts: every EU digital labour platform from **2 Dec 2026** — and, critically, any company using automated tools to allocate work or evaluate contractors, since the algorithmic-management rules apply to all persons performing platform work. Workaround: legal memos and a manual review inbox. Show up: the presumption of employment starts running on that date, non-retroactively, including on existing contracts. Analog: OneTrust, Holistic AI. Death risk: transposition is national and slow; the buyer's cheapest response is to stop using the tool.

**13. eTIMS/e-invoicing "deductibility gateway" for informal-adjacent suppliers.**
A phone-first issuer that produces a compliant invoice a corporate buyer's accountant will accept, in markets where deductibility rather than fines does the enforcing. Hurts: Kenyan traders — **all of them, regardless of turnover**, with KRA auto-validating returns against eTIMS since 1 Jan 2026 — and structurally identical cohorts in Vietnam (cash-register mandate from VND 1bn) and Nigeria (IRN-or-no-payment cascading down supply chains). Workaround: a paper receipt the buyer cannot deduct, so the buyer stops buying. Show up: your customer says "no compliant invoice, no payment." Analog: Wave, Zoho Invoice, Khatabook. Death risk: near-zero willingness to pay; the tax authority ships free tools; this is a European team's weakest ground.

**14. Formal-invoice rails for the informal economy in CEE and the Balkans.**
A phone-first issuer that produces an e-Factura, KSeF or ANAF-valid document with a photo, a voice note and no accounting vocabulary. Hurts: Romanian micro-firms whose <€500k grace period expired **1 Jul 2026** and who now face **15% of invoice value** for a B2B invoice issued outside the platform, plus the coming Polish micro tranche on 1 Jan 2027. Workaround: an accountant who charges more than the fine feels like it's worth. Show up: the first 15% penalty in the neighbourhood. Analog: SumUp's move from card reader to full merchant back office. Death risk: the exact Malaysian scenario — political retreat on micro thresholds; and it competes with an accountant already trusted in the village.

**15. Trade cash-flow: quote → e-invoice → instant payment → verified payee.**
Quote on the van, structured invoice issued on completion, SEPA Instant request-to-pay with **Verification of Payee** (mandatory and free in the euro area since 9 Oct 2025) so the homeowner sees a name match before authorising, and a proof-of-funds check before a large job starts. Hurts: European tradespeople owed £5,901–£6,210 on average across seven invoices, losing 5h20m/week to admin, 93% stressed, with 90% already asking or considering asking for proof of funds. Workaround: half up front, a bank transfer, and a phone call. Show up: because getting paid on the day is the only feature that sells itself. Analog: Powered Now, Tradify, Jobber; SumUp on the payments side. Death risk: card and payment incumbents own the money leg and will bundle; the mandate rails are shared infrastructure, not a moat; unit economics on £150/month software for a one-van business are brutal.

### Which of these I would actually fund

`INFERRED` **#2 (MTD for the unsigned-up)** and **#9 (small-buyer AP)** have the tightest fit between a dated legal obligation, a quantified un-served population, and a workaround that is visibly failing today. **#1** and **#11** are the best-timed narrow wedges. **#6** and **#7** have the deepest moats — the compliance artefact is proprietary the moment you hold it — but the slowest sales. **#13** and **#14** are the largest markets and the worst willingness to pay; Malaysia on 30 August 2026 is the cautionary tale for both.

`SPECULATIVE` The most under-priced fact in this document is that **France's mandate obliges every business to *receive* a year before most must *issue*.** For twelve months, millions of French SMEs will hold a legal duty to accept structured data they have no tooling to read, act on, or reconcile — while their own outbound process stays on PDF. Receive-side tooling is where I would look first, because it is mandatory, unglamorous, and nobody's roadmap treats it as the product.
