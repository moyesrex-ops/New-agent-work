# Non-Finance Arenas of Acute Global Pain — Operator Field Notes

**Compiled:** 2026-08-31. **Bias:** emerging markets, non-finance. **Purpose:** stop the memo over-indexing on tax/fintech.
**Evidence labels:** `OBSERVED` = primary document, regulator filing, audit, or first-party test. `REPORTED` = credible journalism/NGO. `INFERRED` = my synthesis from cited facts. `SPECULATIVE` = my guess, unsupported.

A warning about sourcing before anything else. Roughly a fifth of what a 2026 web search returns on these topics is AI-generated SEO sludge with invented statistics — "ideaproof.io," "liners.com/graveyard," "nlcompass.com," "sentpo.com," and several "startup story" outlets all produced confident numbers I could not corroborate anywhere. I have marked those `REPORTED (low-confidence source)` and leaned on regulator filings, court records, national audits, ILO/WHO/HRW documents, and named journalists wherever possible. If a number below is load-bearing, it came from a primary document.

## 1. Housing anti-fraud

**The pain.** `OBSERVED` FBI IC3's 2025 annual report logged 12,368 real-estate fraud complaints and $275.1M in losses, up from 9,359 complaints / $173M in 2024 — a ~58% complaint jump ([IC3 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [NAR summary, Apr 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/online-real-estate-fraud-climbed-to-275m-in-2025-fbi-says)). Note that IC3's "real estate" bucket blends closing-wire BEC with rental fraud, so it overstates the rental-scam slice.

`OBSERVED` Ireland: An Garda Síochána recorded ~160 accommodation-fraud reports in H1 2025, +22% YoY, €385,000 lost against €617,000 for all of 2024; 66% of victims are under 33 and roughly a third of all reports land in August–September ([Garda press release, Aug 2025](https://www.garda.ie/en/about-us/our-departments/office-of-corporate-communications/press-releases/2025/august/an-garda-siochana-student-accommodation-fraud-warning-august-2025.html)). `REPORTED` Berlin logged 4,862 advance-payment fraud cases in 2025, +32.2% over 2024. `REPORTED` Netherlands: Fraudehelpdesk counted ~250 rental-fraud reports in 2025, about half involving actual loss ([De Telegraaf via Mezha, Dec 2025](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/rental-scams-in-the-netherlands-affect-hundreds-in-2025/)).

Lagos is a different animal — the fraud is *legalized-adjacent*, not anonymous. `OBSERVED` Lagos caps agency + agreement fees at 10% of annual rent and declares inspection, caution, and finder's fees illegal ([LASRERA](https://lasrera.lagosstate.gov.ng/lasrera-initiative.jsp); [Business Post, 2025 ministerial briefing](https://businesspost.ng/general/lagos-insists-tenancy-agreement-commission-fees-remain-10-of-annual-rent/)). Nobody obeys it.

> "Hope you are aware of my inspection fee? Na small money—N25,000." — agent to a Punch reporter posing as a tenant ([Punch, "Rental nightmares"](https://punchng.com/rental-nightmares-how-house-agents-bleed-tenants-dry-with-fraudulent-multiple-inspection-fees/)) `REPORTED`

> "If you paid any inspection fee before they showed you the place, I can tell you for free—it's a scam. The catch is that inspection fee. They have no property in their care to let. So, they take you to a house they are dead certain you will reject." — same article `REPORTED`

> "…fees three times from the same person. Before you even take them to the property they want, you've pocketed N15,000." — realtor Oyindamola Omoniyi, same article `REPORTED`

> "We have been overwhelmed by reports of the activities of these unscrupulous elements making life hard for Lagosians with agreements and commissions that are like asking for an arm and a leg." — Barakat Odunuga-Bakare, Special Adviser on Housing, Lagos State, 2025 Ministerial Press Briefing `REPORTED`

> "There are no strict rules. Anyone can call themselves an agent. We need the government to register all agents so that tenants know who is real." — Beatrice Mwangi, Kilimani estate agent ([Citizen Digital, Nairobi](https://citizen.digital/article/housing-scams-surge-in-nairobis-prime-estates-as-foreign-demand-rises-n371915)) `REPORTED`

> "These fake agents are from the Gumtree and OLX websites. They download property images from sites like Property24 and mark them with lower prices… When a prospective tenant calls, they ask for a deposit before viewing." — Randburg source ([The Citizen, South Africa](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/beware-scammers-will-rent-you-properties-that-arent-theirs/)) `REPORTED`

**Who absorbs the demand today.** LASRERA's public agent registry and complaint form in Lagos (state-run, low-traffic). An Garda Síochána and Fraudehelpdesk (post-hoc reporting, no prevention). Facebook housing groups and WhatsApp estate groups everywhere — that is where the actual matching happens, and where the fraud lives. In Nairobi and Joburg, informal caretaker networks.

**Why verified-listing startups keep dying.** This is the important part, and the answer is not "bad execution."

> "Without fixing the massive housing shortage, landlords—and the agents who play a big role in the rental process—have no incentive to change the model." — TechCabal, on the 2015 cohort of Lagos proptech ([TechCabal, Aug 2024](https://techcabal.com/2024/08/29/spleet-replaced-adetola-adesanmi-over-misappropriation-claim/)) `REPORTED`

> "Growth was slow on the landlords' side. We just couldn't add as many landlords as we wanted to on time." — Adetola Adesanmi, Spleet co-founder, 2022 `REPORTED`

`INFERRED` In a market with a structural supply deficit, verification is a cost imposed on the *short* side of the market. The landlord has ten desperate applicants; being verified buys him nothing. Every verified-listing startup has tried to charge the landlord or the platform and discovered the landlord will not pay and the platform's ad revenue can't fund human inspection. The only side with willingness-to-pay is the tenant, and the tenant is broke and transacts once every 1–2 years. That is a structurally terrible business, and I think most of the graveyard below reduces to it.

**Congregation point for the first 1,000.** Not a website. Lagos: the physical agent clusters at Ojodu-Berger, Ikeja, and Lekki Phase 1 gate, plus the ~50-person WhatsApp "house hunting Lagos" groups. Ireland: the CAO offer week in mid-August, at the student union accommodation desks of UCD/Trinity/UCC. Berlin: the Wohnungsbesichtigung queue itself — literally 60 people on a stairwell.

**Real money?** `INFERRED` Thin on the tenant side, real on the *dispute and recovery* side and possibly on the landlord-side deposit rail. Willingness to pay ₦2,000–₦5,000 for one verification is plausible; willingness to pay a subscription is not.

## 2. Clinic queues, records, medicine authenticity

**Queues.** `OBSERVED` National Hospital Abuja: mean total outpatient time 247.5 minutes (~4 hours); longest single wait was for the doctor at 112.6 ± 86.6 min; records staff were the *shortest* at 20.7 min ([Nigerian Medical Journal, "The Tyranny of Time"](https://doi.org/10.71480/nmj.v67i1.1241)). `OBSERVED` Enugu comparative study: 227.2 min (state teaching hospital) vs 234.0 min (UNTH), with UNTH's *payment* window alone consuming 121 min ([NIJBAS 2025](https://doi.org/10.59298/nijbas/2025/6.3.190000)). `OBSERVED` A 2024 northern-Nigeria primary-care study found a median 159 minutes from arrival to completed registration, with 42.5% dissatisfied at the records window ([preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-6522861/v1)).

> Patients' own suggested fix: "the records staff should desist from collecting bribes from patients in order to place their cards before others." — ABUTH Zaria queuing study `OBSERVED`

`INFERRED` The bottleneck is *payment and registration*, not clinical capacity. That reframes the product: this is a queue-and-cashier problem wearing a healthcare costume. It also explains why "telemedicine for Africa" keeps missing — it solves the 112 minutes with the doctor and ignores the 121 minutes at the cashier.

**Falsified medicines.** `OBSERVED` WHO still publishes "at least 1 in 10 medicines in LMICs are substandard or falsified," ~US$30.5bn/yr, and the underlying estimate is from 2017 — it has not been refreshed ([WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/substandard-and-falsified-medical-products)). Anyone citing "1 in 10" as a 2025 figure is citing 2017. `REPORTED` Africa-specific prevalence is ~18.7%, and reported SF incidents to WHO in 2023 were double 2019; WHA78 in May 2025 *deferred* reform of the Member State Mechanism to at least late 2025, with a vote pushed to WHA79 ([Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/who-delays-falsified-medicine-mechanism-reform-amid-health-crisis/)).

**The cough syrup deaths are the regulatory ignition event.** `OBSERVED` Coldrif syrup, made by Sresan Pharmaceuticals (Kancheepuram, TN), tested at **48.6% diethylene glycol** against a permissible 0.1%; at least 22 children died in Chhindwara, Madhya Pradesh. Tamil Nadu permanently revoked the licence on 13 Oct 2025 after finding **300+ major and critical GMP/GLP violations**; owner G. Ranganathan was arrested; the ED opened a PMLA case ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/health/tamil-nadu-government-completely-revokes-licence-of-adulterated-cough-syrup-firm-shuts-down-company/article70157787.ece); [CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/india/tn-revokes-manufacturing-license-of-sresan-pharma-linked-to-contaminated-coldrif-19713363.htm)).

**NAFDAC's Mobile Authentication Service is a zombie.** `OBSERVED` Two independent 2025–26 first-party tests found it broken. The Whistler sent PINs for four drugs: two got no reply at all, one replied after 26 minutes ([The Whistler](https://thewhistler.ng/nafdacs-mobile-authentication-service-in-decline/)). FIJ tested four NAFDAC tools:

> "Only Sproxil's 38353 and Kezzler's 20966 worked. The earliest response I got from Sproxil came about 10 minutes after I sent the first text. The second response came about an hour after the prompt text." `OBSERVED`

> "Scan2Verify was the worst-performing verification tool among the four. Of the seven medications I tested, it successfully verified only one… None of the other products I scanned returned valid GS1 data." ([FIJ, 2026](https://fij.ng/article/nafdacs-drug-checkers-were-bad-1-year-ago-theyre-still-returning-wrong-expiry-dates/)) `OBSERVED`

`OBSERVED` Peer-reviewed consumer data matches: in Enugu, 93.0% awareness of MAS but only 21.0% "good knowledge" and 40.4% ever-use, with poor network (29.2%) among top barriers ([BMC, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12982-026-01812-6)). And from the supply side: manufacturers bore all MAS costs, five providers each ran their own shortcode, and "interest in MAS waned because of problems and reduced publicity" ([West African Journal of Pharmacy](https://www.wapcpjournal.org.ng/index.php/home/article/view/353)) `OBSERVED`.

`INFERRED` mPedigree/Sproxil's model — the brand owner pays for consumer-facing authentication — failed because the brand owner's incentive to pay decays once counterfeiters simply clone the scratch panel, and because SMS latency destroys point-of-purchase utility. The consumer will not wait 26 minutes at a chemist's counter.

**The new rail.** `OBSERVED` Egypt's EDA made its EPCIS-based national track-and-trace mandatory for imported finished pharmaceuticals on **1 Feb 2026** and for locally manufactured/bulk on **1 Aug 2026** (Decrees 475 and 804 of 2025) — pharmacies must verify a valid tracking code *before dispensing* and refuse unverifiable packs ([ADSERO](https://adsero.me/egyptian-drug-authority-issues-implementation-framework-for-the-national-pharmaceutical-track-and-trace-system/)). That is live as of today. `OBSERVED` Saudi's Wasfaty e-prescribing is mandatory for MOH doctors and pharmacies, but physician and pharmacist studies describe limited EHR integration and only a subset of CPOE best practice ([Frontiers Digital Health 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1812544/full)). `REPORTED` India has no national e-prescription mandate; the Punjab & Haryana HC has directed nationwide electronic prescriptions within two years, and Andaman & Nicobar mandated ABDM-enabled pharmacy MIS in Feb 2026 — that is a court order and a union-territory pilot, not a national rail. Do not model India as mandated.

**Congregation point.** The pharmacy counter, and specifically the *wholesale* counter: Idumota and Ogbo Ogwu (Onitsha Bridge Head) in Nigeria; Bhagirath Palace in Delhi. Also the hospital cashier queue at 7am.

## 3. Vocational hiring with proof

**Who absorbs it.** `OBSERVED` Apna: FY25 operating revenue ₹122 Cr, *down* 4% YoY, net loss ₹50.1 Cr — and critically, only ₹57.1 Cr of that was recruitment revenue; ₹63 Cr was software-development services billed to its own holding company ([Inc42, on MCA filings](https://inc42.com/buzz/apna-narrows-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-cr-even-as-revenue-dips/)). A unicorn whose largest line item is related-party services is not a functioning marketplace. `OBSERVED` WorkIndia: FY24 revenue ₹65.8 Cr, losses ₹30.1 Cr, raised ₹97 Cr Series B, claims FY25 profitability without disclosing numbers ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/workindia-raises-inr-97-cr-to-formalise-blue-collar-hiring-beyond-tier-i/)). `REPORTED` Vahan places ~40,000 workers/month through 2,000+ local agencies, earning ₹5–10 per qualified lead and ₹1,500–3,000 per retained gig placement, ₹5,000–6,000 for manufacturing; 95% of volume is delivery roles ([Founder Thesis interview with Madhav Krishna](https://www.founderthesis.com/p/how-madhav-krishna-built-vahan-into)).

**Why monetization fails.** `INFERRED` Vahan's structure is the tell: it did not disintermediate India's "hundreds of thousands of small recruitment agencies" — it *armed them* and took a cut. Apna tried to disintermediate and its recruitment line is a fifth of a unicorn's expectations. Blue-collar job boards fail to monetize because the employer only pays on outcome, the outcome (retention) is 30–60 days out, monthly attrition in quick-commerce runs 35–40%, and the worker churns back into the funnel — so you are paid once and re-acquire forever.

**Kormo's postmortem is the clearest data we have.** `OBSERVED` Google killed Kormo Jobs on 30 Jun 2022 across India, Indonesia, Bangladesh with 4.5M seekers and 27M lifetime downloads ([Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www2.staffingindustry.com/row/Editorial/Daily-News/Asia-Google-shuts-down-its-Kormo-jobs-site-in-Asia-62393)).

> "They tried to onboard the day laborers from the tea stalls, construction yards, etc. but eventually failed to bring enough of them due to a lack of trust and also a lack of access to the internet or smartphones… Kormo jobs then pivoted to grey-collar workers." `REPORTED`

`INFERRED` That is the whole arena in one paragraph. True blue-collar has no smartphone and no trust; grey-collar has both and is therefore already served by six players. The addressable segment for an app is not the one with the most pain.

**Congregation point.** The labour *nakas* (Mumbai's Andheri and Vashi junctions at 6:30am), Lagos's danfo parks and hardware clusters, and — for grey-collar — the delivery-partner onboarding centre queue, which is where Vahan actually acquires.

## 4. Migrant document bundles and recruitment debt

**The numbers.** `OBSERVED` Nepal's first national recruitment-cost survey (NSO + ILO, 2023 data, published 2026): ~two-thirds of migrants paid recruitment costs; mean Recruitment Cost Indicator **3.3 months of destination earnings** (median 3.7); women 4.5 months vs men 3.2; **unregistered brokers 4.8 months vs registered agencies 3.6**; median cost >NPR 200,000 for GCC and Malaysia versus NPR 16,000 for India ([ILO research repository](https://researchrepository.ilo.org/esploro/outputs/report/The-cost-of-labour-migration-in/995708958602676); [Rising Nepal](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/83224)). `OBSERVED` Bangladesh's 2019 Cost of Migration Survey: **17.6 months** average, 19.1 for men, 5.6 for women ([ILO global study, 2024](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/Global_report_master_combinated_web.pdf)).

> "No worker should begin their migration journey burdened by debt." — Numan Özcan, ILO Nepal Country Director `REPORTED`

**Destination-side mandates now in motion.** `OBSERVED` Japan promulgated Act No. 60 of 2024 on 21 Jun 2024, dissolving the Technical Intern Training Program and creating *Ikusei Shūrō* ("Employment for Skill Development"), operative **1 April 2027**, with employer transfer permitted after 1–2 years — the first time job mobility exists ([Immigration Services Agency outline, rev. Dec 2025](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001438365.pdf)). `REPORTED` The transition traps are brutal and barely documented in English: to reach TITP Stage 3 you must have been in Stage 2 for a year as of 1 Apr 2027, meaning Stage 2 must have started by 1 Apr 2026; and the "good completion" SSW1 exemption is preserved only 当分の間 ("for the time being") with no announced cliff date.

`OBSERVED` Germany's Chancenkarte (§20a AufenthG, live 1 Jun 2024): 11,497 visas issued by 15 Jun 2025 (India 3,721, China 807), rising to 17,489 by Nov 2025 — against an original projection of ~30,000 entries/year. The German federal government has **explicitly stated it does not know** how many holders entered, found qualified work, or left, because the AZR does not record prior Chancenkarte status ([IW Köln study](https://www.iwkoeln.de/studien/jeannette-michaelle-nintcheu-fachkraefteeinwanderung-ueber-die-chancenkarte.html)). `INFERRED` A visa class where the issuing state cannot measure outcomes is a visa class where a private actor could own the outcome data — and sell it to *Länder* and chambers, not to migrants.

**Congregation point.** Kathmandu's Gongabu bus park and the Department of Foreign Employment queue; Dhaka's BMET training centres; the pre-departure orientation seminar (mandatory, captive, 1–3 days); and Manila's POEA/DMW one-stop centre. All physical, all queued, all already paying someone.

**Real money?** `INFERRED` Yes, but it is currently paid to the wrong person. A Nepali worker already pays NPR 200,000+ into this system. Capturing even 3% of that as a legitimate documentation/verification fee is real revenue — but you are competing with a broker who also provides the loan, the flight, and the lie.

## 5. Field-worker payroll and shift proof

**Gulf WPS failure modes, precisely.** `OBSERVED` The ILO Regional Office for Arab States' Nov 2025 review is blunt: WPS coverage excludes public-sector and most domestic workers; in Qatar, workers may hold a salary card without a banking relationship while the employer holds card and PIN; there is no routine access to WPS data for labour inspectors, police, or judiciary outside a couple of countries ([ILO Beirut, 2025](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-11/ILO_Beirut_Wage-Protection_Report_R10.pdf)). `OBSERVED` The UAE requires only **70%** of wages through WPS with up to 10% deductible — so a worker without a payslip cannot tell a legal deduction from theft ([Kalush & Saraswathi, GLMM 2024](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Kalush-Saraswathi-Reforms-without-Rights-Published-2024-09-09.pdf)).

`OBSERVED` Saudi Arabia's Oct 2024 wage insurance scheme: a worker can claim only after **six months** unpaid *and* only if **80% of the company's workers** are similarly affected, capped at SAR 17,500 (~US$4,663), excluding end-of-service ([HRW, 10 Sep 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/09/10/saudi-arabia-migrant-workers-unpaid-for-months)). That design excludes the large majority of wage-theft cases by construction.

> Saudi's HRSD ministry, responding to HRW on Sendan International (≥850 workers unpaid up to eight months, several on Aramco sites): "non-compliance of Sendan International Company was detected through the Wage Protection System, and corrective action was taken in coordination with the company, the affected workers, and their embassies." `OBSERVED`

> Qatar: workers at five labour-supply firms said "management often delayed their wages and told them that they would be paid once the client company pays them" — HRW reviewed contracts specifying 45-day payment terms where payment had been delayed almost a year ([HRW via BHRRC, 14 Dec 2025](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/qatar-labour-supply-firms-implicated-in-theft-of-migrant-worker-wages/)) `OBSERVED`

`INFERRED` WPS detects *missed* payments. It cannot detect *wrong* payments, and the entire fraud has migrated into the gap between "amount deposited" and "amount owed." The missing artifact is not a payment rail — it is a worker-held, tamper-evident record of hours worked and rate agreed. That is a shift-proof problem, not a payroll problem.

## 6. School fees and parent communication

**Kill this darling first.** `OBSERVED` Teachmint, the best-funded Indian school-software company, reported FY25 operating revenue of ₹74 Cr — and Entrackr's read of the MCA filing is explicit that **sales of the Teachmint X / X2 digital boards "and related services" were Teachmint's sole revenue source in FY25**, with inventory procurement at 35.7% of total cost ([Entrackr, Oct 2025](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)). Losses ₹46.6 Cr. `INFERRED` India's flagship school SaaS company monetizes by selling hardware into schools. The SaaS did not retain; the whiteboard did. `REPORTED` Classplus crossed ₹350 Cr sales and cash-flow positive in 2025 — but Classplus sells to *creators and coaching educators*, not to low-cost private schools, and its portfolio wins (Testbook, Gyan Live) are test-prep. The low-fee K-12 school is not the customer in either success story.

`OBSERVED` Bridge International Academies: at IFC's investment it ran 211 schools with 57,000+ students in Kenya; in Feb 2024 it separated from NewGlobe into an independent Kenyan non-profit, terminating all financial engagement, and now serves ~22,000 pupils ([CAO Compliance Appraisal, Aug 2024](https://www.cao-ombudsman.org/sites/default/files/downloads/Kenya-Learn-Capital-04-CAO-Compliance-Appraisal-Report-Aug-2024-ENG.pdf); [TNX Africa, 2026](https://www.tnx.africa/events/article/2001556112/golfers-gather-in-nairobi-to-back-education-nutrition-programmes-for-vulnerable-children)). The for-profit low-cost-private-school chain converted to charity. That is a verdict.

`INFERRED` Why school SaaS churns: the proprietor's ARPU is bounded by the fee (often US$5–15/month/child), the software's value accrues to the *administrator* who is often the owner's relative working unpaid, and the one function with real willingness-to-pay — getting parents to actually pay — is seasonal (term start) and directly substitutable by a WhatsApp broadcast. Retention dies in month four of the term.

## 7. Agri offtake, input credit, and EUDR forced traceability

**This is the strongest tailwind in the entire memo, and it has a date.**

`OBSERVED` Regulation (EU) 2025/2650, published Dec 2025, sets EUDR application at **30 December 2026** for large and medium operators plus all downstream operators and traders, and **30 June 2027** for natural persons and micro-enterprises. Deforestation-free means no clearance after 31 Dec 2020, evidenced by geolocation ([European Commission Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation)). `REPORTED` The 13 Jul 2026 Delegated Act finalized Annex I scope and the EUDR Information System, closing the last legislative questions; the Commission's May 2026 simplification review confirmed no further extension.

**What it forces on cooperatives.** `OBSERVED` ISEAL's cocoa case study: ~80% of Ghanaian land is customary and undocumented; in Côte d'Ivoire only ~4% of rural land carries a certificate or title. Farms >4 ha or in high-risk areas need polygon mapping ([ISEAL](https://isealalliance.org/node/5360)). `REPORTED` Ghana has ~800,000 cocoa smallholders on typical 2–4 ha plots; COCOBOD says the Ghana Cocoa Traceability System is fully operationalised and the Forestry Commission launched a FAO-aligned 2020 forest-cover map for cocoa areas ([GhanaWeb](https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/business/Cocoa-industry-steps-up-EUDR-readiness-efforts-2036443)).

`INFERRED` As of 31 Aug 2026 there are **four months** to the large-operator deadline, and field practitioners put polygon-mapping-at-scale at 12–18 months from a standing start. That gap is the opportunity: not the national traceability system (governments built those), but the last-mile cooperative that has 3,000 farmers, a paper ledger, and a buyer who will refuse the 2026/27 crop without a DDS. Requirements are unglamorous and specific: offline-capable GPS polygon capture on cheap Android, cooperative-level aggregation, and an export in the shape TRACES wants.

**Congregation point.** The cooperative weighing station at harvest, and the *pisteur*/purchasing-clerk who already visits every farm. `INFERRED` You do not recruit 3,000 farmers; you recruit the co-op manager who is about to lose his buyer.

## 8. Used goods and used-car authenticity

**Kill a second darling: Kavak is not a corpse.** `OBSERVED` Kavak exited Peru and Colombia (announced Nov 2023) and Brazil (Oct 2023, ~2,000 laid off), and took a down round to $2.2B from an $8.7B peak — 75% off ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/after-peru-colombia-exit-used-car-startup-kavak-refocuses-mexico-exec-says-2023-11-09/); [Contxto](https://contxto.com/en/funding/kavak-raises-127-million-round-and-ends-up-with-75-lower-valuation/)). `REPORTED` But Kavak's own newsroom reports ~120,000 transactions in 2025, +40% YoY, inventory rotating ~60% faster, and **December 2025 as the first month of consolidated global profitability**, followed by a ~$300M Series F led by a16z in Feb 2026 ([Kavak newsroom](https://news-room.kavak.com/closing-2025-strong-starting-2026-even-stronger)) — company-sourced, so discount accordingly.

> "Kavak is not for everyone. This is a highly demanding place where we cannot compromise an average or relative performance to what's happening in the market." — Federico Ranero, COO, to Bloomberg Línea during the 2022 Brazil layoffs `REPORTED`

`INFERRED` The inventory model was not the fatal flaw; the withdrawal of cheap capital during a five-country simultaneous expansion was. The lesson for a memo is the opposite of the usual one: *asset-heavy trust businesses in used goods can work, but only single-market and only when capital is patient.* That is a bad fit for a seed-stage thesis, which is itself the useful conclusion.

**Nigeria.** `OBSERVED` Cars45 raised $120M+, saw its founders leave to start Autochek, was hit by mismanagement allegations, and was absorbed by Jiji in Jun 2021; the entire marketing team resigned within a week of the merger, five of eleven management members left, and the tech team was laid off with technology moved to Ukraine ([Olumuyiwa Olowogboyega, *Not A Deep Dive*](https://www.notadeepdive.com/p/a-wave-of-exits-at-cars45); [TechMoran](https://techmoran.com/2021/06/28/jiji-acquires-struggling-cars45-operations-in-kenya-ghana-and-nigeria/)).

> "Every car should be treated like a potential investment. You would not buy land without a survey. Why buy a car without an inspection?" — Damilola Ojurongbe, Head of Technical Services, Cars45, on a 22% YoY rise in inspection requests in Q1 2025 ([The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/features/executive-motoring/used-car-inspection-rises-by-22-in-q1/)) `REPORTED`

**Escrow.** `OBSERVED` Jiji, the dominant African classifieds platform, has no integrated escrow; payment happens off-platform. A cottage industry of third-party escrow apps (TrustAm on Rubies MFB rails, Troco) has grown to fill it, explicitly advertising support for "Jiji, Facebook, Olist, eBay…". `INFERRED` The fact that escrow is a *separate app* rather than a Jiji feature after a decade tells you Jiji has decided escrow is negative-margin for a free-listing business. That is a durable gap, but the third-party escrow app has a fatal acquisition problem: the buyer only learns it exists after being scammed once.

## 9. Safety tools on bad networks

`OBSERVED` Peer-reviewed reviews of Indian women's-safety apps converge on the same failure list: "most apps are heavily dependent on stable internet and GPS connectivity, limiting their effectiveness in rural or low-network areas," plus crashes, battery drain, delayed police response, and language barriers ([IJIP, Sep 2025](https://ijip.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/18.01.262.20251303.pdf)). `OBSERVED` A 2026 review of four Android safety apps names the deeper problem: "All four apps assume the victim has their phone, can reach it, and can interact with it… Community-based features only work if there are enough users in the area to make the network meaningful" ([IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/reviewing-android-based-women-safety-apps-a-look-at-what-works-what-doesn-t-and-where-we-go-next-ijertv15is041541)). `OBSERVED` The 2025 academic response is offline-first, multi-trigger design (SMS fallback, BLE mesh, shake/voice activation) — Sanraksha, SafeHer, DivineShield.

`INFERRED` Government apps have distribution (Disha in Andhra Pradesh: 1M+ downloads) and no retention. Nothing here monetizes B2C. The only real payer is an *employer* with night-shift liability — BPOs, hospitals, quick-commerce fleets — and in India that payer is created by law, not by conscience. This is a B2B2C compliance product mislabelled as a consumer safety product.

## 10. Last-mile government paperwork

**The single most important number in this document.** `OBSERVED` India's Comptroller and Auditor General audited CSC 2.0 and reported to Parliament that **86% of rural Village Level Entrepreneurs earned less than ₹500 per month**, implying an 8+ year payback on a ~₹50,000 setup; 7.5–18.5% earned zero; fewer than 3% earned above ₹3,000/month; average state-level earnings ranged ₹76–₹499; 46.49% of VLEs recorded fewer than 100 transactions annually. Of 51 lakh grievances 2020–24, 44.9% took more than two days and 11% more than 15 days. Only 4,913 VLEs nationally earn above ₹10,000/month ([CAG audit via MediaNama, Apr 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-cscs-inactive-cag-audit-exposes-structural-gaps-rural-digital-services-network/)). `OBSERVED` There are 4,07,122 functional rural CSCs as of 30 Jun 2026 ([Lok Sabha AU2766](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2766_2IKhMd.pdf)).

`INFERRED` This obliterates the standard "arm the agent" thesis for India. You cannot sell software to a network where 86% of nodes earn less than $6/month. The agent economy is real but its *unit* is not viable; only the 1.2% of high-volume urban-adjacent agents are a market.

**Nigeria contrasts sharply.** `REPORTED` NIN enrolment is officially free; the official DoB-correction fee rose from ₦16,340 to ₦28,574 in May 2025; applicants report being charged ₦45,000–₦55,000 by agents and middlemen. NIMC fired 100+ front-end partners, revalidated 7,167 agents, and claims a 40% reduction in extortion ([Nairametrics, Sep 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/09/06/nimc-agents-in-abuja-accused-of-collecting-money-from-applicants-for-nin-date-of-birth-falsifications/); [The Nation](https://thenationonlineng.net/nimc-fires-over-100-partners-for-extortion-others/)).

> "Whoever is saying 'I want to reduce my age and they are collecting N45,000 from me' is a pure lie because nobody in our office will do modification for you. If you go somewhere and ask somebody to help you, both the giver and receiver of the bribe are guilty under the law." — NIMC official, quoted by Nairametrics `REPORTED`

`INFERRED` Nigeria's agent economy has *ten to twenty times* the per-transaction take of India's, because the service being sold is partly illegal. That is exactly why a legitimate product cannot compete on price — it can only compete on the buyer's fear of being caught, which is not a demand curve I would build on.

## 11. Anti-counterfeit parts, materials leakage, micro-insurance, shop energy

**Do micro-insurance claims actually pay?** `OBSERVED` Kenya's IRA data show Turaco Microinsurance received 13,224 claims in Q2 2025 (KSh 119M) and **rejected 6,632 — 50.2%** (KSh 66.9M), paying 5,765 worth KSh 37.7M ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/micro-insurer-rejects-half-of-claims-on-fraud-detection-5166812)).

> "The figures do not mean Turaco is rejecting valid claims. The quarter-on-quarter variation is largely explained by stronger fraud detection and prevention measures… around 85 percent are due to clear and transparent reasons — such as the claimant not being on cover at the time of loss, claims submitted for individuals not enrolled, or benefits not included under the policy." — Turaco to Business Daily `OBSERVED`

`INFERRED` Read that answer carefully. Turaco is saying half its claims come from people who did not understand what they bought. Whether that is fraud or mis-selling, it is the same product defect: embedded micro-insurance is sold by a distribution partner whose incentive ends at attachment. The Kenyan micro-insurance sector's loss ratio is 57.7% on KSh 2.17bn gross premiums, with two firms holding 99% of the market — so this is a two-company market, not a sector. `REPORTED (low-confidence source)` Widely circulated figures of "30M farmers, 62% payout ratio, 48-hour payouts" for Pula/ACRE trace back to an AI-generated data-story site; I could not verify them against Pula or ACRE primary reporting, and I would not put them in a memo.

**PAYGo solar.** `OBSERVED` Peer-reviewed: the share of PAYGo companies incurring write-offs and receivables-at-risk *increased* 2020–2023, and repossessions are becoming more prevalent even in strong markets like Rwanda ([Nature Comms Sustainability, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44458-026-00078-y)). `REPORTED` GOGLA's PAYGo PERFORM v3 push exists precisely because "current credit metrics can be 'gamed'" and Portfolio-at-Risk and collection rates "can mask deeper issues" ([NextBillion](https://nextbillion.net/ending-vicious-circle-paygo-solar-how-companies-investors-can-move-sector-toward-paygo-2-0/)).

> "The biggest risk that still deters mainstream investors is the portfolio quality risk… Many off-grid companies struggle to quantify and explain the portfolio quality data to investors in terms they understand." — Swaroop, on Sun King's $286M securitization and d.light's $50M green bond `REPORTED`

`INFERRED` The bankable artifact in PAYGo is no longer the lamp; it is the *audited repayment cohort*. Sun King and d.light have securitized; everyone below them cannot, because securitization arrangers want ≥5,000 units per product with weekly-reported cohort data and most companies cannot produce it.

**Construction materials leakage.** `REPORTED` Indian practitioner estimates put untracked material loss at 2–6% of total material value on sites without formal receipt verification and consumption reconciliation, against materials being 60–80% of direct project cost. `OBSERVED` Nigerian academic work frames site pilfering as a corruption problem spanning pre- to post-construction, with material theft the most common incident and 51.9% of contractors rating technology interventions "highly effective" but adoption blocked by training and funding ([Taylor & Francis, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/15623599.2024.2352177)). `INFERRED` The loss occurs in three undocumented gaps — delivery-to-receipt, receipt-to-store, store-issuance-to-consumption — which is a photo-and-signature problem, not a camera problem.

**Ranked honestly, only three clear 7+:** EUDR co-op compliance (#1), the Egypt pre-dispense mandate (#3), and Japan's 2027 transition (#8), with shift-proof (#2) and the hospital cashier (#4) close behind. Every one of those five is powered by a *dated legal obligation on a specific named party*, not by user pain. That is the actual finding of this exercise, and it is uncomfortable: across eleven arenas of genuinely acute suffering, the demand that converts without a distribution miracle is almost always regulatory, and the sufferer is almost never the payer.

**Three darlings I am killing publicly.** (a) "Arm the last-mile agent" is dead in India — 86% of CSC VLEs earn under ₹500/month and you cannot build a SaaS business on nodes that poor. (b) "Verified listings fix rental fraud" is dead everywhere with a housing shortage, because verification taxes the short side of the market. (c) "Asset-heavy trust models can't work in emerging markets" is *also* wrong — Kavak reached consolidated profitability in Dec 2025 and raised $300M in Feb 2026. The failure mode was five-country expansion on borrowed money, not the inspection bay.

**What I could not verify and would not put in a memo:** Pula/ACRE's "30M farmers, 62% payout ratio, 48-hour payouts"; WorkIndia's "$15M ARR, blockchain-verified credentials"; Germany's "€40M annual rental fraud" and the Netherlands' "€1.75bn." All four trace to SEO or AI-generated sources with no primary citation. WHO's "1 in 10" is real but dates to 2017 and has not been refreshed — cite it with the year attached or someone will catch you.
